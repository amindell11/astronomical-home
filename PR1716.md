# PR #1716 (task/elite-inertia-scale @ cfb99ca): two diagnoses

## 1. SectorHuntPlayModeTests.SeeingTheHeroAgainWhileSearching_ReturnsToTheChase
The test is phase-sensitive and flaky on main too. Inertia doesn't cause it.

- Plain test, run locally: passes on the branch, and passes with main's inertia (new Ship.cs line commented out). CI's two failures (49.7, 53.6) didn't reproduce locally.
- Sweep, re-spot delayed 0–55 ticks × hero on either flank (24 cases): **branch 11 pass / 13 fail, main 12 / 12.** Same failure rate.
- Mechanism:
  - `SeekingHunt` sets `spotFillSeconds: 1`. One second after the re-spot the meter fills, `Sound` fires, and the patrol is released. Its navigator command goes to exactly 0 (thrust, strafe, yaw) at t = 1.00 s in every failing case, and it coasts away for the remaining 7 s, which is how the gap reaches ~50.
  - So the test only passes when the patrol closes 4 units inside that first second. That depends on its drift at the re-spot, not on how fast it turns. In its search orbit the nose sits ~90° off its velocity, so it is flying sideways.
  - Closing speed at the re-spot: branch passes +8.8 m/s on average, fails −4.6; main passes +12.9, fails −10.9.
  - The nose reaches the hero within 20° in 0.35–0.48 s in every case.
- Inertia effect, observed but not causal:
  - True yaw inertia is 130 on the branch vs 232 on main, and the model inertia equals the true value in both.
  - The MPC owns yaw throughout; the servo never does, because the patrol is unaware and has no gun aim.
  - In the search, RMS yaw rate is 157 vs 104 deg/s and sign flips are 1.9 vs 2.0/s: the same ~1 Hz wobble, about 1.5x the amplitude. That shifts the trajectory phase only.
- Fix (in the test, not the AI): give this test's hunt a longer alarm budget.
  - With `spotFillSeconds: 3` the sweep passes 24/24, but two cases took 3.1 s and 3.9 s to close, and that would trip the test's later `Assert.IsFalse(hunt.Alarmed)`. Use ≥ 5.
  - Putting the hero on the velocity side instead of the nose side: 17/24. That helps, but isn't enough on its own.
- Side note, out of scope: once released, the patrol coasts with a zero command.

## 2. Bulwark ships never let the aim servo take yaw
Neither the obstacle-threat veto nor the hull radius is the cause. The ram keystone opts out of servo aim by design.

- `RamDash.FliesNavigatorHeading => !Braking` (Ships/Keystones/Kits/RamDash.cs:95) is true whenever the ram isn't braking, idle included.
- For those ships `AICommander` (line ~192) passes `AimReference.None` to `Navigator.ComputeCommand`. All Bulwark prefabs carry Bulwark_Dash (RamDash).

Fight vs a Crimson aggressor, 1500 ticks:

| ship | servo owns | gun aim live | latched (AIM weight 1.0) | vetoed | mean aim error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bastion elite | 0.03 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.05 | 42° |
| Bastion minion | 0.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.03 | |
| Bastion rival | 0.05 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.05 | |
| Shoal elite | 0.89 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.07 | 24° |
| Veil elite | 0.97 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.00 | 23° |

The hull radius used in ObstacleThreat averages 1.45 on the Bulwark elite, 1.02 on the minion and 1.20 on the rival, against 1.59 on the Shoal elite, so Bulwark is not the outlier.

**Candidate change** (measured on a local scratch build, not committed): `FliesNavigatorHeading => RamLive`, which keeps the servo out only during the live ram.
- Servo owns yaw: elite 0.90, minion 0.97, rival 0.88. The controls are unchanged (Shoal 0.95, Veil 0.99).
- Elite mean aim error: 42° → 26°.

This is a design ruling, not a bug fix. "The kit attacks along the heading the navigator flies" (#1062) made servo-free Bulwarks deliberate, and #1629 opened only the brake to the servo. I didn't check how servo yaw during idle affects ram setups.
