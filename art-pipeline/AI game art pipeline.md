# Build Blender-First, Let AI Explore Upstream

As of September 2026, the solo and indie developers getting good results from AI art use it **upstream of the shipped pixels**. It does divergent exploration, reference-anchored edits, paint-overs of real engine or Blender renders, masks, decals and icons. Geometry that ships stays authored in Blender, and the art is judged in-engine. Nothing in the research shows a well-received indie game built on large amounts of player-visible AI art. The documented record is mostly backlash, and it has a measurable cost: Steam games that disclose AI drew about **53% fewer first-month reviews** in 2025. Your candidate stack is the right shape but needs three fixes. First, "GPT Astra" is **GPT-6 Astra**, a reasoning LLM OpenAI shipped on September 3–4, 2026. It is not an image model, it is three weeks old, and nobody has reported on using it for art direction yet. Second, the original "Nano Banana" API model **shuts down on October 2, 2026**. Nano Banana 2 should replace it, paired with OpenAI's GPT Image 2.5, which currently leads blind-vote image-edit rankings by a wide margin. Third, you already drive a repo, a Unity MCP server and contact-sheet review through Claude Code agents. So the strongest setup has **one orchestrator that owns a versioned art bible and reference pack in git**. A second LLM is useful as an outside critic, not as a co-driver. You have already been working this way without naming it: the Vanguard livery work (hand UVs plus AI paint masks) and a fully procedural HDR skybox both follow the "Blender-first, AI on top" pattern that the evidence favors.

## Players punish visible AI, so winners keep it in exploration

The backlash cases share one trigger: AI output reached players looking inconsistent, off-style or undisclosed. *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* lost its Indie Game Awards Game of the Year and Debut wins because a few generative-AI placeholder textures slipped into the launch build. They were patched out within days ([Comics Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/indie-game-awards-rescind-clair-obscur-expedition-33s-wins-for-gen-ai-use/)). *Vapor World: Over The Mind* shipped AI cutscenes that didn't match its hand-made visuals, got negative reviews, and removed every one of them in a hotfix ([TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/news/vapor-world-over-the-mind-ai-cutscenes)). Larian got backlash just for saying it used genAI for *concept art*. It then committed to no genAI art in Divinity ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/larian-studios-draws-harder-line-on-ai-clarifies-stance-in-reddit-ama/1100-6537296/)). The Larian case matters for a solo dev who plans to talk about their process publicly: the reputational risk reaches past what ships.

The numbers point the same way. Game Oracle's analysis of about 10,000 paid Steam releases in 2025 found that AI-disclosing games got about **53% fewer first-month reviews** than matched peers. Their average score among titles with 100+ reviews was **84.6% versus 88.3%** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/)). Quantic Foundry surveyed 1,799 gamers: **85% were negative on AI in games**, and they were most negative about AI artwork specifically ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)). Developers are split too. GDC's 2026 survey found **52% think genAI harms the industry**, while 36% use it personally, mostly for research and brainstorming ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)).

The rules reward the same posture. Steam's disclosure covers content "that ships with your game and is consumed by players", and it explicitly excludes behind-the-scenes efficiency tools ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). US copyright protects human selection, arrangement and modification of AI material, but prompting alone isn't enough ([USCO Part 2](https://copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). The Supreme Court left the human-authorship rule in place when it declined *Thaler* in March 2026 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). A pipeline where AI explores and a human finishes the work in Blender does three things at once. It keeps most assets outside disclosure scope, it maximizes what you can own, and it avoids the "doesn't match the game" failure that sank Vapor World. Two caveats. Store and marketing art is plausibly "consumed by players", and the Steam doc doesn't settle that. And a published interpretation reading concept art as exempt is a Substack's reading, not Valve's text ([Production Alchemist](https://www.productionalchemist.com/p/steam-ai-disclosure-rules-2026-what-indie-devs-need-to-know)).

The most relevant lesson for someone who wants to be the director comes from *Echoes of Somewhere*. Its solo developer found the 2023-era tools "very bad at taking direction" and ended up rewriting his story to fit what the AI produced. In other words, the model steered the director ([Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/feature/519/echoes-of-somewhere-how-a-solo-developers-game-takes-center-stage-in-the-ai-cont)). Reference-image-capable models have partly fixed the "can't take direction" problem, but the process risk remains. The counter is structural. An **art bible** means a few opinionated visual pillars, shape-language rules per faction, and a palette defined by *roles* rather than a list of hex values ([Nasty Rodent](https://nastyrodent.com/game-art-bible/)). A fixed **reference pack**, a versioned set of up to about 14 images fed with every generation, does the steering, instead of ever-longer prompts ([MetaFluxTech](https://metafluxtech.medium.com/10-pro-nano-banana-tips-for-high-quality-asset-production-93c25b7385b2)). The one practitioner rule that recurs everywhere is "quantity doesn't equal quality; human curation is essential" ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/indie-dev-ai-art-guide)).

## GPT Astra is OpenAI's new reasoning model, not a paintbrush

"GPT Astra" is **GPT-6 Astra**. OpenAI released it to approved users on September 3, 2026 and publicly on September 4. It is marketed for professional work, coding and computer/web navigation, and it lists "video game development" among its use cases ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-6_Astra)). It rolled out across ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo) through Enterprise and in Codex, it has a "Pro" variant, and it is billed as about **2x faster at computer use** ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/04/openai-releasing-major-upgrade-to-chatgpt-and-codex-with-gpt-6-astra-details-here/)). The API lists a 1.05M-token context at **$10/$50 per million input/output tokens** ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6-astra)). Neither source mentions native image generation. Inside ChatGPT, images come from a separate family: GPT Image 2 (April 2026) and ChatGPT Images 2.5 (September 8, 2026) ([Wikipedia: GPT Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT_Image); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-images-2-5/)). Google DeepMind's "Project Astra" is a different product, and nothing suggests you meant it. Two things couldn't be confirmed: whether GPT-6 Astra accepts image input for critique (OpenAI's launch page returned 403), and whether it has any track record on art direction. At three weeks old, it doesn't.

That shapes what "driver" should mean here. The job the driver actually does looks like this. It keeps the art bible current. It turns the bible into a stable prompt preamble. It pre-screens contact sheets against a written rubric. It sends work to image APIs, Blender and Unity. And it files the *why* of each pick to the tracker. Most of that is tool orchestration, and you already have an orchestrator wired to your repo, your Unity MCP server and your capture skills. Running a second LLM as a parallel "creative driver" in a separate chat creates two sources of truth for the style rules, which is exactly the drift the art bible exists to stop. Best use of GPT-6 Astra: an **outside critic and second opinion**. Send it the same contact sheet and rubric and see whether its ranking disagrees with your primary agent's. It is also a convenient front door to ChatGPT Images 2.5 when you want to sketch in-chat (the new @Sketch feature turns drawings into references). One more reason not to make any vision LLM the final judge: its taste isn't yours. The stop condition for any agent's self-critique loop should be "meets rubric, hand to director", never "the model likes it". This is a process inference; I found no independent evaluation of vision-LLM art critique.

## Nano Banana needs an upgrade and a partner before October 2

"Nano Banana" now names four Google models. If your scripts call `gemini-2.5-flash-image`, they **stop working on October 2, 2026** ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). **Nano Banana 2** (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`) is Google's recommended workhorse. It goes up to 4K and is the only Google model with dedicated **style-reference slots (3) alongside 10 object and 4 character references**, which is exactly the plumbing for keeping dozens of assets in one style ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). It costs **$0.067 per 1K image and $0.101 at 2K, with a 50% batch discount**. Pro is $0.134, 2 Lite $0.034, and no model has a free image tier ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Every output carries an invisible SynthID watermark.

Google isn't the leader on independent boards, though. On LMArena's September 21 rankings, OpenAI holds the **top three spots in both text-to-image and image-edit**. GPT Image 2.5 "Sunburst" scores 1526 in editing against 1390 for Google's best (Nano Banana Pro), a gap of more than 130 points ([arena.ai image-edit](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/image-edit); [text-to-image](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text-to-image)). That matters most for your "go deep on one piece" mode, which is mostly *edits*: change only the dorsal fin, repaint this livery band, add battle damage. Game-specific comparisons all come from vendors that resell both models, so treat them as directional. They agree that GPT Image wins on UI mockups, layout control and text, and Nano Banana wins on photographic lighting, atmosphere and native 4K ([SpriteCook](https://www.spritecook.ai/blog/gpt-image-2-comparison); [PixVerse](https://pixverse.ai/en/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-2)). SpriteCook's most useful finding is humbling: run-to-run style variation "can be bigger than the gap between the models". That argues for fixed reference packs over model-shopping.

| Task | First pick | Why | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cheap silhouette/mood exploration | Nano Banana 2 (batch) or 2 Lite; Midjourney V8.x if you want pure aesthetics | Low cost; MJ keeps moodboards and `--sref` ([guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/midjourney)) | Vendor/docs |
| Precise "change only this" edits on a pick or a Blender render | GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst | #1 image-edit by a wide margin | Independent, general-purpose |
| Set-wide style consistency | Nano Banana 2 with a fixed style/object reference pack | Dedicated style slots | Docs |
| HUD/menu mockups over game captures | GPT Image 2 / 2.5 | Layout and text control | Vendor |
| Lighting/atmosphere paint-overs of look-dev frames | Nano Banana 2 | Photographic light, 4K | Vendor |
| UI icon families | Recraft V4 (native SVG) | Only mainstream vector-output model ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/blog/recraft-v4)) | Vendor/partner |
| Local/LoRA pipeline later | FLUX.2 [dev] in ComfyUI | Open weights, 10 references; commercial use needs a BFL license ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-2)) | Vendor |

One gap stands out. **I found no source that tests hard-surface spaceships or orthographic ship sheets across models.** All the turnaround tooling is built for characters. The one sourced tip is to force "orthographic projection" or "flat view" in the prompt ([PrintPal](https://blog.printpal.io/nano-banana-for-3d-model-generation-best-prompts-tips-and-workflows/)), which implies the models default to perspective. Since you already have Vanguard in Blender, skip the problem: **render ortho and three-quarter views from the actual model, and do AI work as edits of those renders.** The front, side and top views then agree by construction, and every concept inherits the real proportions.

## Blender stays the spine; AI meshes are for rocks and props

Image-to-3D has improved a lot. Hunyuan3D 3.x, Rodin Gen-2, Meshy 6, Tripo and Microsoft's MIT-licensed TRELLIS.2 all output textured PBR meshes. But topology is still the weak point, and most output is dense triangle soup that needs retopology ([3DAI Studio](https://www.3daistudio.com/blog/hitem3d-vs-meshy-vs-tripo-comparison), an aggregator). The quad-mesh modes that just shipped, like **Tripo P2.0 on September 21, 2026**, come with vendor claims their own coverage tells teams to test ([AiCybr](https://aicybr.com/blog/tripo-p2-native-quad-mesh-ai-3d-generation)). The polycount consensus on hard-surface is that AI is good for inspiration and mockups, "not yet a tool for production" ([polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/233254/what-are-your-thoughts-on-ai-on-hard-surface-modeling)). That thread is probably older than the current model generation. For a space-combat game, AI meshes are clearly useful for **asteroids and debris**: they're organic and noisy, seen at speed, and decimate well. Distant stations and background props are next. Crisp bevels, symmetry and panel lines on a hero ship will look soft next to Vanguard. You can try this already: Unity's built-in 3D Object Generator uses **Hunyuan 3D 3.0 Pro by default** and produces a prefab for "simple, single-part props" ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.ai.generators@1.6/manual/3d-generator-overview.html); [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/unity-ai-3d-object-generator)). Your session's Unity MCP also exposes `generate_model` and `generate_image` tools, backend unconfirmed. Two traps: self-hosted Hunyuan weights **exclude the EU, UK and South Korea** from their license territory ([GitHub LICENSE](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)), and TRELLIS.2 officially needs **Linux and a 24 GB+ NVIDIA GPU** ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)).

The agentic side of Blender is where you get the most for your effort. Anthropic's official Blender connector (April 28, 2026) exposes Blender's full Python API as an MCP server ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-creative-work)). The community BlenderMCP adds Poly Haven, Sketchfab and Rodin/Hunyuan hooks, but warns that its arbitrary-code tool can delete scene data ([GitHub](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)). Practitioners agree on the shape: treat the coding agent as "a fast junior technical artist", good at "repetitive, measurable, and easy to inspect" work ([best-games.io](https://best-games.io/blog/codex-claude-code-blender-mcp-game-development-guide)). It is imprecise at spatial placement and needs rounds of rendered feedback ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-blender-mcp-real-world-performance)). Your skybox is the model case for this. `art/tools/skybox/skybox_merged.py` is a committed, seeded, headless bpy script that emits true scene-linear HDR. It beats the dedicated AI option: Blockade Skybox AI costs $24–$140/mo and reviewers still flag seams and coherence problems ([The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/skybox-ai)). Generic image models also produce the classic equirect wrap-seam and "pinwheel" pole artifacts ([panoramagenerator](https://panoramagenerator.com/blog/skybox-formats-explained)), which show up when players roll freely in 6DOF. Keep the procedural skybox. If you want AI nebula shapes, bring them in as a colour layer multiplied by HDR intensity, not as a replacement. The same "scripts beat live pokes" rule should cover the cleanup chain for AI props (merge by distance, decimate, auto-UV, bake the dense mesh onto a low-poly). Quad Remesher is the usual paid upgrade; it keeps creases above about 30° and runs in seconds ([SuperRendersFarm](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/quad-remesher-blender-retopology)). It should also cover export: FBX with Apply Scalings "FBX All", -Z forward, Y up ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/blender-to-unity-export-checklist)).

On texturing, your Vanguard approach is the best fit the research turned up. Full AI projection texturing (StableProjectorz, AGPL since January 2026) admits it "sometimes struggles with hard-surface industrial geometry" ([SuperRendersFarm](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/stable-projectorz-3d-texture-generation-ai)). **Hand-authored UVs plus AI-generated masks and decal sheets composited in UV space** keep the hard-surface edges crisp while AI supplies the variation. What your current setup lacks is discipline around it. `textures/livery_mask.png` and `livery.svg` have diverged edit dates, and `experiments/2026-09-04-ai-livery/` holds four review variants and an edit guide, but no file in that folder records which prompt, references or model produced the AI masks. For hero surfaces, bake normals and roughness from real geometry. AI-inferred normals from a single albedo leak baked lighting. For asteroid rock, an AI tileable texture with triplanar mapping is enough. Stay away from research-only models like Ubisoft's CHORD, which bars commercial use ([Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/ubisoft-open-sources-the-chord-model)).

## UI, VFX and shaders reward text formats and capture loops

UI shows the concept-versus-implementation split most clearly. Nano Banana Pro now renders legible button text. But it has no grasp of design systems or cross-screen consistency, and its grid is "suggestive rather than pixel-perfect". Raw.Studio's workflow treats mockups as targets to **rebuild by hand against a design system**, not as assets to slice ([Raw.Studio](https://raw.studio/blog/ui-design-with-nano-banana-pro)). Rebuilding is now cheap for you. Unity shipped an official Claude Code plugin on September 9, 2026 with a `/ui-uitk` skill for UXML/USS ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/unity-plugin-for-claude-code)). UXML/USS is HTML/CSS-like text that diffs cleanly in PRs. A 2026 comparison puts UI Toolkit ahead for menus and data-heavy screens, and uGUI ahead for Animator- or Timeline-driven gameplay HUD feedback ([h-idris.com](https://h-idris.com/blog/unity-ugui-vs-ui-toolkit.html)). The piece that holds this together is a small **token sheet**: 6–8 role colours (hostile, friendly, warning and so on), a type scale, spacing and line weights, kept as USS custom properties. Agents reference those tokens instead of writing literal values, and the same values feed image prompts and Recraft palettes. Figma is optional. Its MCP server and the Figma-to-UXML converters ([FigmaToUnity](https://github.com/TrackMan/Unity.Package.FigmaToUnity)) earn their place only if you'll actually hand-tweak layouts in Figma. For icons, Recraft's native SVG plus agent post-processing (normalize viewBox, force stroke width, `currentColor`) enforces consistency more reliably than prompting. Unity 6.3+ imports SVG natively for UI Toolkit ([Unity Manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.4/Documentation/Manual/ui-systems/work-with-vector-graphics.html)). For HUD style, readability beats decoration ([Sunstrike](https://sunstrikestudios.com/en/blog/HUD_design_in_games/)). Your two poles are *Everspace 2*'s clean overlay ([Unreal interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/everspace-2-delivers-a-handcrafted-universe-brimming-with-space-combat)) and *Elite Dangerous*'s diegetic cockpit ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Dangerous)). Test any HUD hue against captures of your actual nebula, since a thin cyan HUD can disappear into a teal sky.

VFX and shaders have the thinnest evidence and the clearest method. The best published practitioner result is from Coplay, the team behind the Unity MCP ecosystem. Their agent built Shuriken particle effects, deliberately skipping Shader Graph, and reached "decent but rough" quality only after three changes: adding a retrievable **recipe library**, generating masks and sprites to spec, and a **visual feedback loop** that captures the densest frame. They also note that stills miss motion problems ([Coplay](https://coplay.dev/blog/ai-vfx)). Agent-authored Shader Graph is unreliable today. One Unity MCP's Shader Graph tools wrote identical broken 809-byte assets ([GitHub #18](https://github.com/AnkleBreaker-Studio/unity-mcp-plugin/issues/18)), and Coplay's MCP had Shader Graph only as an open request in July 2026 ([GitHub #1255](https://github.com/CoplayDev/unity-mcp/issues/1255)). So have agents write **HLSL `.shader` files derived from one known-good URP template**, grounded in references like [Cyanilux's URP shader guide](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/urp-shader-code/). Check each one with a compile-and-console read, a fixed-camera capture, and a player build to catch variant stripping ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-unity-urp-shader-not-rendering-build)). Flipbooks are the biggest visual lever for explosions. The proven sources are Blender sims, EmberGen (indie perpetual license $299.99, per [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/03/jangafx-releases-embergen-1-0/), which may be outdated) and CC0 packs. AI-video-to-flipbook has no practitioner evidence yet. Finally, the look-dev loop: capture screenshots, grade toward a target, then implement the grade in engine. That's standard indie practice ([IndieDB](https://www.indiedb.com/features/lut-color-correction-in-video-games-devlog-24)). AI fits at the "target frame" step, repainting a real capture, and the agent then translates the gap into bloom, tonemapper, emissive and gradient values.

## Conclusion

The real fork isn't Nano Banana versus GPT Image, or Claude versus GPT-6 Astra. It's where AI sits relative to the source of truth. Every approach that fails in the evidence lets generated images become the reference the game has to match. Every approach that works makes the *game*, meaning Blender renders, in-engine captures and approved assets, the reference that generation has to match. For you, that means the most important asset in the pipeline isn't a model subscription. It's the reference pack built from your own renders, which gains a new image every time you approve a piece. That pack is also what keeps a deep dive on one ship from pulling the rest of the fleet off-style. Refresh it through a **lineup sheet**: every approved asset at the same scale, lighting and camera, reviewed as one contact sheet.

The other finding is that your existing engineering discipline carries straight over into art direction. Committed scripts over live pokes, briefs on issues, gates you have to cross explicitly, and provenance you can audit are the same controls the art-process literature says solo directors lack. So the workflow below adds few new tools. Mostly it applies those controls to art, with a generation budget and a written question per decision, so the models can't turn it into a slot machine.

## Recommended staged workflow

Terms used below: a **director gate** is a checkpoint that only you can pass. Agents iterate freely between gates but can't cross one. A **provenance sidecar** is a small JSON file next to each generated image, recording the prompt, reference-pack version, model and version, seed, parent image, and a status of `exploration`, `placeholder` or `approved-reworked`.

| Stage | What happens | Tools | Director gate | Output in repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Foundation (≈1 week)** | Write art bible v0: 3 pillars, shape rules per ship class/faction, palette-with-roles, lighting rules, a "never" list, a 64 px silhouette-readability rule. Build reference pack v1 from Vanguard ortho/3-quarter renders, skybox frames and the best 1–2 of the existing `art/concepts/` PNGs. Adopt provenance sidecars. Migrate any `gemini-2.5-flash-image` call to NB2. | Claude Code (bible and pack upkeep), Blender render script, `game-capture` stills | Approve pillars and pack v1 | `art/bible/`, `art/refpack/v1/`, sidecar convention |
| **1. Look-dev lock** | Pick tonemapper and HDR/bloom once. Capture a fixed look-dev frame set (combat, shield hit, engine trail, explosion against the nebula). Generate 8–12 repaints of those frames. Pick a target; the agent turns it into URP volume and emissive parameters; recapture side by side. | Unity MCP, NB2 (lighting), GPT Image 2.5 (precise edits) | Approve target frame and the matched in-engine frame | Look-dev scene, volume profile, approved target frames added to pack |
| **2. Per-asset concept loop** | Open a tracker issue with one written question ("which silhouette reads as heavy interceptor?"). Budget: one sheet of 12–16 grayscale silhouettes, at most 2 rounds. Agent pre-screens against a rubric (silhouette, pillar fit, palette rules, buildability, lineup fit, AI tells). You pick by elimination. Colour/livery variants are *edits of Blender renders*, not fresh generations. | NB2 batch or Midjourney (divergent), GPT Image 2.5 (convergent edits), optional GPT-6 Astra second-opinion ranking | Pick from sheet; approve blockout in-engine at gameplay distance | Issue with the pick and why; blockout .blend |
| **3. Production** | Hero ships: modeled in Blender with agent help on scene setup, naming, bevel/weighted-normal passes and bakes; livery as UV-space AI masks and decals on hand UVs. Asteroids/debris/background props: Hunyuan (Unity generator), Tripo or Meshy, then the committed cleanup and bake script. One committed FBX export script plus a Unity AssetPostprocessor. | Blender connector/BlenderMCP, committed bpy scripts, Unity MCP | Final approval in the lineup sheet | `art/tools/` scripts, graduated asset in `Assets/Visuals/` |
| **4. UI** | HUD/menu mockups over real captures → extract decisions → USS token sheet → agent-authored UI Toolkit menus (HUD tech per open decision below) → capture and compare to the mockup. | **5. VFX and shaders** | A recipe doc per effect class (muzzle, impact, explosion, shield hit, engine trail); Shuriken first; flipbooks from Blender sims, EmberGen or CC0; HLSL from one URP template. Review as multi-frame contact sheets or clips, never single stills. | Unity MCP, Blender, `game-capture` clips | Approve from a clip at gameplay camera | Recipe docs, shader template, flipbook library |
| **6. Ship hygiene (continuous)** | A pre-build check fails if any `placeholder` or `exploration` sidecar asset is referenced by a shipping scene. A disclosure ledger lists every AI-derived pixel that ships. | CI / editor test | Review ledger before any store submission | Check script, ledger |
