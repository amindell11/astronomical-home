Image 1 is the design to refine, and it is the one to stay closest to: a short laser bolt made of chunky, uneven, flat cel-shaded bands (white-hot core, pale cyan, bright cyan, electric-blue rim). Its jaggedness lives in the band shapes themselves: each band's outline is an irregular, chunky, torn edge, and the bands overlap unevenly. Keep exactly that: the edge language, the palette, the flat hard-edged bands.
Refine it into one short, compact blaster bolt, a projectile rather than a beam: roughly six to seven times longer than it is thick, travelling left to right, with a slightly fuller head on the right and a short tapering tail on the left.
Image 2 is the main inspiration for the bolt's presence: a thick white-hot core filling about half the bolt's width, a saturated blue sheath and a soft blue bloom around it. Take its intensity and the realism of that bloom, but keep the body hard-edged and cel-banded like Image 1.
Images 3 and 4 are style references from our game's effects: flat hard-edged tone bands, a thin darker rim, a hand-painted anime-VFX feel.
Image 5 is the game's style frame: a painterly blue nebula backdrop with cel-shaded ships. The bolt has to sit in that world and read clearly against it.
Do not add separate lightning squiggles or filaments floating outside the bolt; all the electric feel comes from the torn, irregular band edges. Edges are irregular, never a uniform saw-tooth.
Render a single bolt, horizontal and centred, about half the frame width, on a pure flat black background (it becomes an additive game sprite). No other objects, no text, no ships, no scenery.
