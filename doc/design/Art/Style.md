# Art direction

**Bold, hand-drawn spacecraft against luminous, layered space, with quiet futuristic instruments.** The feeling is adventurous, daring and curious: the thrill of taking a capable ship into a dangerous, unfamiliar galaxy. Space should invite exploration. Deep shadows add weight and drama without turning the world nightmarish or oppressive.

## Form, line and light

Use **hand-drawn lineart with dimensional, simplified shading**. Confident, selectively weighted ink contours define silhouettes and important overlaps. Broad local colors and a few decisive light/shadow planes give objects volume; small highlights pick out a canopy, an edge or an engine. Detail earns its place by clarifying the form.

The influence from [[Hades]] is the sculptural shadow design, expressive contours and vivid light against quieter surfaces. Keep that graphic confidence while preserving an expansive, adventurous space-fantasy mood. Avoid glossy showroom rendering, intricate mechanical surface detail, dense panel lines and uniform outlines around every feature.

The result must hold up in **2.5D: ships roll and asteroids spin while retaining the drawn appearance**. The reference images establish the look, not a proven rendering technique. Silhouettes, shading and surface marks must make sense beyond one camera angle.

## Ships and structures

Ships have distinct silhouettes and independent liveries: ivory with orange, red or cool blue/periwinkle accents, grounded by dark mechanical recesses and black canopies. Preserve each source design's proportions and wing arrangement when translating it into volume; adding a generic pointed nose can erase its identity.

Surfaces feel **used, maintained and cared for**. Faint scuffs, slight wear at service seams and restrained exhaust staining are second-look details. Paint stripes and edges remain intact. Broad color areas stay readable; conspicuous paint chips, exposed-metal patches, scratch clusters and pervasive grime push the ships too far toward battle damage.

Build hangars from a few broad deck and wall planes, chunky supports, sparse alignment marks and simple lamps. A clear cast shadow grounds the ship. Keep architectural detail at the same level of simplification as the gameplay objects.

## Space, color and depth

Nebulae are **broad, overlapping wisps with softly quantized color**—visible tonal steps softened selectively at their boundaries. They have an authored, layered structure between featureless airbrush gradients and busy brushwork. Avoid watercolor-like clusters, stippling, scratchy paint, sharp fractal edges and noisy photographic clouds.

Luminous cobalt, cyan, periwinkle and violet establish the combat reference. Teal against plum and the green gas giant show how different places can have their own palettes within the same style. Leave calm dark spaces around important silhouettes; the background supports the action.

Asteroids read as irregular sculpted masses with broad angular facets, grouped shadows and a few inked cracks. Planets use immense curved horizons, sweeping atmospheric bands and selective storm shapes. Both belong to the same illustrated world as the ships.

## Effects

Explosions have a compact pale-hot core and sharp amber/orange flame shards that make the impact legible. Shield fields remain transparent enough to read the ship, with a luminous rim. Use restrained, localized bloom on impacts, engines, shields and lights; preserve crisp nearby silhouettes and dark-space depth.

## Interface

The UI is **flat, minimal and precise**: thin off-white strokes, dark translucent navy backing, subtle dividers, generous spacing, narrow clean typography and modest outlined actions. Let the environment remain visible behind it. Ship paint does not dictate UI color; orange belongs comfortably on a hull without becoming the interface theme.

Communicate through simple fills, segments, pips and compact symbols, with short text where it helps a decision. Glyphs should suggest unfamiliar spacecraft instruments: interrupted arcs, offset strokes and detached geometric marks. They need distinct silhouettes and consistent meanings; literal guns, missiles, heraldic shields and X markers undermine the distant-galaxy feeling.

The preferred map uses fine double rings, sparse ticks and a few softly glowing contacts. Avoid bulky glowing panels, ornamental circuitry, heavy borders and excessive labels.

## Staging and next review

The intended pairing is a physical hangar for the opening departure and an in-space loadout view between sectors, sharing one UI language. The first conveys preparation; the second preserves the feeling of an ongoing voyage.

Still to validate: the look during ship rolls and asteroid rotation, and symbol readability at gameplay scale. Next review should test those in motion. These concepts do not settle ship-swapping rules or equipment availability.
