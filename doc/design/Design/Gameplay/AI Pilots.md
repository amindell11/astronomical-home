## What are we aiming for?

(a) Hard to beat, (b) fun to fight, or (c) convincingly competent: flies with visible purpose and makes believable mistakes, win or lose?

> [!quote]
> (c), with (b) as a test.

## What should a fight feel like?

> [!quote]
> When I picture what I want combat to feel like I picture Starbuck fighting Scar in her Viper, or Anakin outmaneuvering the missiles in the opening of Revenge of the Sith, or Obi-Wan outsmarting Jango in Attack of the Clones.

## What is wrong today?

> [!quote]
> No piloting competence. They seem to twitch around and fly timidly, bad at holding aim, terrible at anticipating the player, also no awareness of other ships (when there's multiple they just sit next to each other and shoot at you), also not very dynamic. They just get in range and then sit there.

## Should the player be able to read a pilot?

> [!quote]
> Definitely should be readable.

## How do pilots differ from each other?

> [!quote]
> Distinct pilots with skill range.

## How do pilots act around each other?

> [!quote]
> Fix spacing and bearing spread first, but be careful about authoring a rule that says stay spread just for the sake of it. That's not what we want necessarily and can look forced.
>
> They should treat teammates as obstacles in MPC. Crashing is bad.

## How do flying and shooting fit together?

> [!quote]
> There are weapons where the strategy is very complex and nuanced, like grenades, which require a strategy that takes flight into account, or railguns, which require the pilot to plan ahead and think about flight and gunning as a holistic strategy (start charging while zeroing in, or when anticipating being in LOS at fire time). These strategies are hard to learn as a player currently, so it makes sense they would also be hard to program. But it also points to potential design flaws if these weapons are so hard to figure out how to use well. But that could also be depth. Not sure.

## How do we know it's good?

> [!quote]
> I'll be the playtester for now. I have found that numbers don't really quantify any of this stuff well. It really needs playtests to characterize AI behavior.

## What is deliberately later?

> [!quote]
> Enemy pilots for now, one day wingmen will be in there. Fleet is a far stretch.
>
> Nerfing the senses and reflexes is way later. That's not really a problem until we can make it smart with perfect information.
