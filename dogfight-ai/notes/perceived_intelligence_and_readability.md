# Perceived Intelligence, Readability and Fairness in Combat AI (and how it translates to a top-down ship duel)

Evidence tags used throughout: **[Measured]** = a study or playtest with reported numbers; **[Designer report]** = a shipped-game developer describing what they did and saw (talks, Game AI Pro chapters, postmortems), often backed by internal playtests whose data is not published; **[Lore]** = widely repeated design wisdom without a cited test behind it; **[Community]** = player forum perception; **[Secondary]** = I only saw a summary or transcript digest, not the primary talk/text. Most of this field is designer report. Controlled studies are few, small, and mostly in non-shooter genres.

## 1. Halo (Butcher, Griesemer, Isla): illusion of intelligence, "tougher = smarter", 30 seconds of fun, readable states

### Takeaway
Bungie's GDC 2002 slides contain the only widely cited numbers. In two playtest conditions, raising enemy toughness raised the share of players calling the AI "very intelligent" from 8% to 43%, and "not intelligent" fell from 20% to 0%. The slides give no sample size, method or statistics, and the tougher condition was also rated "about right" on difficulty far more often (92% vs 52%). That makes it a suggestive designer-run survey with a confound, not a controlled finding. The rest of the talk is a design framework: be intelligible, interactive and unpredictable, show the AI's state, don't hide it, and don't cheat on perception. That framework is designer report, but other studios have echoed it heavily.

### Cited Findings
- Talk: Chris Butcher & Jaime Griesemer, "The Illusion of Intelligence: The Integration of AI and Level Design in Halo", GDC 2002. Bungie split the work: design owns a "3 minute scope" (racial personalities, strategic purpose) and code owns a "30 second scope" (intelligent decisions, instant reactions). [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Individual-level design goals: "Imitating the Player's capabilities", "Transparent thought process", "Racial personality". Group-level goals: "Obvious strategic goals", "Clear racial roles". The three umbrella goals were "Intelligible / Interactive / Unpredictable". [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- They wanted the player to feel three things. **Impressed**: the AI reacts to the player with surprise, anger and awe. **Fooled**: limited knowledge and predictable reactions. **Thwarted**: a breaking point (flee in terror, berserk, retreat, defensive state). [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- "Discarded: Randomness." Unpredictability was meant to come from "Reactive AI" (unpredictable player, situations and reactions) and "Analog Reactions" (position, timing), not from dice rolls. [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Knowledge model: "Discarded: Complete Model". They used "'Real' Perception", "No cheating" (vision, hearing, touch, "ESP"), selective memory and persistent state, and the AI "Can be fooled". [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Readable states: "Making the AI Intelligible — Discarded: Hidden States — Inform the Player: Language, Posture, Gesture — Focus of Attention — React to the Player — Dialogue — Animation — Communication of Intent." [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- **The playtest numbers.** Under "Playtest Feedback — Lifespan — Smarter = Tougher — Tougher = Smarter":
  - Weak Enemy Playtest: too hard 12%, about right 52%, too easy 36%. Very intelligent 8%, somewhat intelligent 72%, not intelligent 20%.
  - Tough Enemy Playtest: too hard 7%, about right 92%, too easy 0%. Very intelligent 43%, somewhat intelligent 57%, not intelligent 0%.
  - The slides do not state the number of testers, how toughness was changed (the "Lifespan" heading suggests health/shields), the survey wording, or any statistical test. [Measured, but undocumented method] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- The design response listed next to those numbers: "Consistent Challenge", "Negative Reinforcement — Discourage boring tactics — Reward experimentation". "Things to Avoid: Subtlety, Looking Broken, Insufficient Challenge". "Things to Refine: Communication, Animations, Engagement Distances". [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Racial personality was implemented as per-race action-selection "black boxes": "Grunts flee easily", "Elites seek cover if hurt", "Jackals carry shields". Positioning used weighted "firing points" scored on line of sight, distance to target, cover, friends/enemies, vehicles and grenades. [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Combat dialogue was triggered from decisions and stimuli (hurt, death, saw enemy, throw grenade, seek cover), with nearby characters able to reply: 57 events, 166 dialogue types, 12 speaking characters, 5,147 recorded lines. The slide also says "Used for flavor only". [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Griesemer (2011) says the "30 seconds of fun" quote came from this AI and level-design talk. The 30-second scale meant "where to stand, when to shoot, when to dive away from a grenade". The real model is "a 3-second loop inside of a 30-second loop inside of a 3-minute loop that is always different", and "No 30 second stretch of Halo is ever repeated; the missions are constantly changing the context on you." [Designer report] — [Engadget interview with Griesemer](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html)
- Griesemer on combat feel: the AI "waits to see what you will do, how hard you will push, and then it pushes back at just the right resistance". Halo enemies "won't usually" chase a player behind cover, which lets a defensive player recover. [Designer report] — [Engadget interview with Griesemer](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html)

### Inferences
- The "tougher = smarter" effect is most plausibly about **exposure time** (a longer-lived enemy shows more behavior) and/or **appropriate difficulty** (the tough condition was also "about right" 92% of the time). It is not evidence that raw hit points create perceived intelligence. The practical lesson that survives the confound: an enemy has to live long enough for the player to see it make several decisions.
- **Ship-duel translation (lifespan):** applies directly. With one to three skilled enemies, each pilot should survive several exchanges: shields and hull tuned so a duel lasts long enough to see a few maneuvers, a retreat and a re-engage. If enemy pilots currently die (or kill) in one or two passes, players cannot perceive intent whatever the AI is doing.
- **Ship-duel translation (racial personality → pilot archetype):** one simple per-archetype rule set ("flees when hull < X", "seeks asteroid cover when shield down", "always boosts out after a railgun shot") is the same cheap trick Halo used. It works without animation because the rule shows up in the flight path.
- **Ship-duel translation ("no cheating" perception):** the developer's AI currently has perfect information. Halo's explicit position was a limited, foolable knowledge model, because being fooled is part of what makes the player feel clever. Asteroid occlusion and a last-known-position memory are the natural ship equivalents.
- **Ship-duel translation (pushback calibrated to the player's push):** Griesemer's "pushes back at just the right resistance" and "won't usually pursue behind cover" map to a pilot that presses when the player is aggressive or exposed and backs off when the player disengages into asteroids. That is the opposite of the current "get in range and sit there".

### Gaps
- No published sample size, protocol or statistics for the Halo playtests. I found no replication of "tougher = smarter" with controls for difficulty and exposure.
- I did not find a primary source for the widely repeated claim that Halo enemies ramp their accuracy up the longer the player stays out of cover. A search snippet described it as a "card trick", but I could not trace it to a Bungie talk.
- Damian Isla's Halo 2/3 talks (behavior trees, "Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI", GDC 2005) were not retrieved. I have no verified Isla quotes on perceived intelligence.

## 2. F.E.A.R. (Jeff Orkin, "Three States and a Plan"): what made players call it smart; evidence that announcing intent matters

### Takeaway
Orkin says plainly that the squad behaviors players praised were largely an illusion. Each soldier planned on its own (a 3-state FSM plus a GOAP planner), and squad "coordination" came from proximity grouping and, above all, from scripted 2–3 line dialogues that announced state and intent ("If the AI didn't say it, it didn't happen"). One small controlled study (AIIDE 2023) supports the core claim: contextual dialogue let simple NPC behavior match complex behavior in player enjoyment. Evidence strength: strong designer report plus one small experiment in a different genre (top-down stealth).

### Cited Findings
- Talk: Jeff Orkin, "Three States and a Plan: The AI of F.E.A.R.", GDC 2006. Agents use a finite state machine with only three states (Goto, Animate, Use Smart Object). A* searches over actions (GOAP) as well as paths. [Designer report] — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013282/Three-States-and-a-Plan); summary of the talk at [TechEnclave](https://techenclave.com/threads/f-e-a-r-s-ai-demystified.12465) [Secondary]
- Orkin, as quoted in the summary: "we actually did not have any complex behaviors at all in F.E.A.R. Dynamic situations emerge out of the interplay between the squad level decision making, and the individual A.I.'s decision making, and often create the illusion of more complex squad behavior than what actually exists!" NPCs are grouped by proximity, and individuals are unaware of their squad-mates. [Designer report, Secondary] — [TechEnclave summary](https://techenclave.com/threads/f-e-a-r-s-ai-demystified.12465)
- Orkin, "Combat Dialogue in FEAR: The Illusion of Communication" (Game AI Pro 2, 2015): "If the AI didn't say it, it didn't happen... There is no point in expending significant effort implementing complex AI if the player doesn't notice it." Reviews and forum chatter singled out the coordinated squad behaviors, not GOAP. [Designer report; anecdotal evidence from reviews] — [Orkin, Game AI Pro 2 ch.2](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter02_Combat_Dialogue_in_FEAR_The_Illusion_of_Communication.pdf)
- Mechanism: single barks became multi-character dialogues, canned 2–3 line exchanges triggered "when enough squad members were in proximity and some criteria was met." For example, when a soldier is shot, someone else asks "What's your status?" and the injured one replies "I'm hit!" or "I'm alright!". This tells the player they scored a hit, sells squad cohesion, and hints at enemy health. [Designer report] — [Orkin, Game AI Pro 2 ch.2](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter02_Combat_Dialogue_in_FEAR_The_Illusion_of_Communication.pdf)
- Dialogue can explain inaction. An AI that doesn't reposition under fire looks broken, but "Get out of there!" / "I've got nowhere to go!" turns that into awareness. AIs tracked regions where comrades had died and refused to enter them, voiced as "Advance!" / "No f***ing way!", which turns a stalemate into a story. [Designer report] — [Orkin, Game AI Pro 2 ch.2](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter02_Combat_Dialogue_in_FEAR_The_Illusion_of_Communication.pdf)
- Dialogue can imply behavior that doesn't exist. The last surviving soldier yells "I need reinforcements!", and players credit any later enemies to him: "We never wrote any code for the AI to call in reinforcements, but the reviews said we did!" [Designer report] — [Orkin, Game AI Pro 2 ch.2](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter02_Combat_Dialogue_in_FEAR_The_Illusion_of_Communication.pdf)
- **Controlled evidence:** Al Enezi & Verbrugge (AIIDE 2023), top-down stealth prototype, 2×2 design: NPC behavior (simple vs complex) × dialogue (simple vs contextual). With simple dialogue, players enjoyed complex behavior more. With contextual dialogue, simple behavior matched complex behavior in enjoyment. Sample size was not visible in the abstract page. [Measured] — [Al Enezi & Verbrugge 2023, AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27512)
- The Last of Us used the same layering: "Dialog in particular allowed the AI to communicate the decisions they make to the player, so that the NPCs could appear to be as intelligent as they sometimes were." Many decisions were driven by focus-test video. [Designer report] — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Rabin's "head look": an AI that spends a second looking at each of two enemies before attacking one, and later glances back at the other, "can telegraph deep intelligence to the player for something that the AI instantaneously chose." [Designer report/Lore] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)
- Ocio Barriales (2021): "Use smart barks that can convey a feeling of intelligence, but do not overuse them." Reactions should be "readable and consistent", because players learn the systems and brains spot repetition. [Designer report] — [Ocio Barriales, Game AI Pro Online 2021 ch.11](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIProOnlineEdition2021/GameAIProOnlineEdition2021_Chapter11_You_had_me_at_AAAAHHH_On_the_importance_of_reactions_in_game_AI.pdf)
- Poivet, Pelachaud & Auvray (arXiv, Dec 2025) ran two experiments in a military shooter with questionnaires plus behavioral and physiological measures. "Disrupting players' initial expectations influences their assessment of NPCs, with coherent and consistent design reinforcing expectations." Participant counts were not visible in the abstract. [Measured] — [Poivet et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07388v1)

### Inferences
- What the evidence supports: announcing state and intent raises perceived intelligence and enjoyment for a given underlying behavior, and can stand in for behavior that was never built. What it does not show: whether *nonverbal* announcement (visual tells) has the same effect. All the controlled evidence is about dialogue.
- **Ship-duel translation (radio chatter):** this fits the touchstones (BSG, Star Wars) and the genre, so voiced or subtitled comms ("Breaking left, cover me!", "He's on my six!", "I'm overheating, pulling out") are the single highest-leverage, lowest-code technique in this whole note. With one to three enemies, a call-and-response between two pilots is exactly Orkin's dialogue pattern. Even text callouts with a pilot portrait work if voice isn't budgeted.
- **Ship-duel translation (head look):** in this game, facing is decoupled from velocity, so **the ship's nose is its head**. A pilot that yaws to track the player while drifting sideways, or briefly swings its nose toward a second threat before committing, is doing Rabin's head-look with no animation needed. Strong candidate.
- **Ship-duel translation (explaining inaction):** when a pilot holds off (overheated, waiting for a boost cooldown), a one-line callout ("Guns hot, need a second") turns "timid AI" into "cautious pilot". This is cheap insurance for the current timidity complaint while the underlying behavior is fixed.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that tests visual-only intent signaling (no dialogue) on perceived intelligence in action games.
- Sample size and effect sizes for Al Enezi & Verbrugge 2023 were not retrievable from the abstract page.
- F.E.A.R.'s "players called it smart" rests on reviews and forum chatter as reported by Orkin. No survey data.

## 3. Attack tokens, kung-fu circle, attack slots, aggression budgets

### Takeaway
Every studio that documented its system (DOOM 2016, God of War 2018, Spider-Man, Kingdoms of Amalur, The Last of Us, and FreeSpace 2 for space fighters) caps simultaneous threats with a shared budget. The stated reasons are fairness (no unreadable damage spikes from many attackers at once), readability (you can see who is attacking) and difficulty tuning (scale the budget, not each enemy). The known cost is enemies visibly "waiting their turn". The documented fixes are to let non-token holders do purposeful support roles, or to let them shoot but deliberately miss in visible ways. Evidence: consistent designer report backed by internal playtests. No published controlled study.

### Cited Findings
- **DOOM (2016)** (Kurt Loudy, "Embracing Push Forward Combat in DOOM", GDC 2018, via Tommy Thompson): each attack type (melee, ranged, charged) has a limited number of tokens. Demons must request a token before attacking and release it afterward. Demons can steal tokens from each other if better placed. Token counts vary with difficulty. Accuracy is deliberately reduced when the player moves at top speed and rises as the player slows. Enemies won't shoot explosive barrels near the player. Demons can't start new attacks during a glory kill. [Designer report, Secondary] — [Game Developer: "Cyber-Demons: The AI of DOOM 2016"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)
- **God of War (2018)** (Mihir Sheth, "Evolving Combat in God of War for a New Perspective", GDC): "only two enemies could be aggressive simultaneously". Aggressive enemies are held in a "crescent-shaped positional constraint in front of Kratos" so they stay visible, while non-aggressive ones circle. Off-screen indicator arrows are "red for incoming attacks and white for idle enemies". Playtest: "Players felt anxious when enemies went off-screen, which hindered their confidence and offensive gameplay", so on-screen enemies were made to leave the screen only through player action. [Designer report, Secondary (transcript digest)] — [Video digest of Sheth's GDC talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)
- **Marvel's Spider-Man** (Adam Noonchester, GDC AI postmortem): "an attacker token was essentially passed around to different members of an enemy group to control the pace". After a successful dodge, an idle timer gave the player a few free swings. They "made off-screen enemies take longer to fire ranged weapons in general to give the in-game 'Spidey Sense' a fraction of second longer to alert players." Early playtesters called enemies "cheap". [Designer report] — [Game Developer: Designing AI for Marvel's Spider-Man](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/designing-ai-to-do-anything-a-spider-can-in-i-marvel-s-spider-man-i-)
- **Kingdoms of Amalur: Reckoning** (Michael Dawe, Game AI Pro):
  - The "Kung-Fu Circle" (one attacker at a time) "may be too strict" for fast combat. Their "Belgian AI" puts a grid of 8 slots around the player with a **grid capacity** (limits how many can engage, weighted by creature size, e.g. soldier 4, troll 8, player capacity 12) and an **attack capacity** (limits which attacks can run at once, e.g. troll charge 6 vs club 4).
  - Engaged creatures wait in an outer "approach" circle and step into the inner "attack" circle only to attack. Creatures without slots stand in front of empty slots outside the circle, which "made for natural flanking behavior without any creature explicitly needing to know about any other creature."
  - Slots are released right after an attack and locked during it. Difficulty scales the two capacities only. Individual, creature-wide and global cooldowns stop the same attack type from stacking.
  - [Designer report] — [Dawe, Game AI Pro ch.28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- **The Last of Us** (Travis McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2): "it was only necessary for one NPC to be shooting the player at any given time; all other NPCs could spend their time taking cover, flanking, etc."
  - A global Combat Coordinator hands out roles (Flanker, Approacher, Investigator, StayUpAndAimer, OpportunisticShooter) via RequestRole/AcknowledgeRole. The Flanker role goes only to the single best-rated candidate each frame. The OpportunisticShooter role guarantees at least one NPC is always shooting, and it "instantly" interrupts any animation to fire, because in earlier playtests rushing players were "almost completely untouched".
  - Lethality came from full-body hit reactions that take control away: "it was the loss of control that most affected players."
  - [Designer report] — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Token critique and fix** (Sergio Ocio Barriales, Game AI Pro 3):
  - Gating firing with a token "could yield potentially unbelievable behaviors, such as AIs being at perfect spots to shoot and hit the player but not even trying to do so because they are clearly waiting for their turn."
  - The fix: everyone may shoot, but only the token holder's shots can hit. A global timer sets the gap between hits from a base of 0.5 s times rule multipliers: distance <5 m halves it; crouching ×2; in cover ×2; player facing away >170° ×2; player running toward the AI ×0.5.
  - The token goes to the "most relevant" agent, scored on distance, target exposure, archetype, whether it is under attack, and time since it last held the token.
  - [Designer report] — [Ocio Barriales, Game AI Pro 3 ch.33](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter33_Using_Your_Combat_AI_Accuracy_to_Balance_Difficulty.pdf)
- **FreeSpace 2 (space fighters).** The open-sourced FS2 engine has per-skill-level `$Max Player Attackers` and `$Max Missiles Locked on Player` profile values. When the number of ships attacking the player reaches the cap, `ai_maybe_limit_attackers` calls `remove_farthest_attacker`. [Primary source code] — [FS2 Open ai_profiles.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai_profiles.cpp); [FS2 Open aicode.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Lars Lidén's "Artificial Stupidity: The art of making intentional mistakes" (AI Game Programming Wisdom 2, 2004, pp. 41–48) is the origin usually cited for "kung-fu style" fights. Secondary summaries say he described only about two of many enemies effectively attacking at once, so the player feels powerful. [Lore/Designer report, Secondary] — [Wikipedia: Artificial stupidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_stupidity)

### Inferences
- The shared rationale across these sources is about the *damage arrival process*. Players judge fairness by whether they could have seen the hit coming and whether hits come one readable source at a time, not by total enemy competence.
- **Ship-duel translation (lethal-weapon token):** strongly applicable even at one to three enemies, and directly relevant to "the player dies quickly when it does get a shot". Use a single "kill-shot token" for the high-lethality options (charged railgun release, missile lock-and-fire, close autocannon burst). Only one pilot holds it at a time, with a minimum gap after the player was last hit. Others may harass with low-damage laser fire, ideally with visible near-misses (Ocio).
- **Ship-duel translation (roles instead of waiting):** TLOU's coordinator maps cleanly onto two or three pilots. Attacker (holds the token, presses), Flanker (best-rated angle off the attacker's line), Interceptor/Cutoff (sits on the player's likely escape vector), Overwatch (stays at range and holds lock). This directly answers "ignore each other" and "get in range and sit there". Each non-token pilot has a job the player can see, which avoids Ocio's "clearly waiting for their turn" failure.
- **Ship-duel translation (Belgian slots):** Amalur's trick of standing in front of an empty slot to get flanking for free carries over as a few angular slots around the player (e.g. ahead, both beams, astern). Pilots claim slots, which keeps them from stacking on one bearing and produces crossfire geometry with no explicit teamwork code.
- **Ship-duel translation (off-screen rule):** in a top-down camera, an enemy off-screen is invisible. Copy Spider-Man (off-screen ships wind up slower) and God of War (edge-of-screen arrows, red when winding up an attack). God of War's playtest anxiety finding suggests enemies shouldn't voluntarily leave the screen mid-duel.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved for Batman: Arkham or Assassin's Creed combat AI token/attack-order rules (only fan/review summaries surfaced). Not included.
- God of War details come from a third-party transcript digest, not the GDC Vault recording.
- No published playtest data quantifying fairness gains from any token system.

## 4. Telegraphing, tells, commitment and punish windows

### Takeaway
Designer consensus is strong and consistent. An attack is fair when it is preceded by a perceivable anticipation (visual, audio, ideally both), when the cue-to-threat mapping is consistent, and when attacks leave a vulnerable recovery the player can punish. Fighting-game frame data (startup / active / recovery) is the most formal version. I found no controlled study that isolates telegraphing's effect on perceived fairness or intelligence. Player complaints about Elden Ring's input-reading and delayed attacks show what breaks: AI that reacts to the player's button press on the first frame, or that varies timing specifically to catch the player's reaction, gets called cheap. Evidence: designer report plus community perception.

### Cited Findings
- Mike Stout (Game Developer, 2015): "If players don't understand the questions they are being asked, they actually can not play your game." "Before the attack, we need a small delay. The purpose of this delay is to tell the player 'Okay, here I come.'" Projectiles stack channels: animation, SFX, VO, VFX and rumble, e.g. a Mega Man X-style charge with gathering particles, a "vacuum-cleaner-in-reverse" sound and a flash. On fairness: "If the sword comes down and the player takes damage, it's not because they didn't know what they were being asked to do." [Designer report/Lore] — [Stout, "Enemy Attacks and Telegraphing"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing)
- Monster Hunter director Kaname Fujioka: early monsters have "relatively simple attacks, easy tells—when it does this, you know it's going to charge at you, you get out of the way". Monster complexity escalates across the game. [Designer report] — [Vice interview](https://vice.com/en/article/the-developers-of-monster-hunter-explain-what-its-like-to-build-monsters)
- Punch-Out!!: "the game gives you a lot of hints about effective timing of punches". Bald Bull's charge is signaled when "a light flashes to the right in the audience", which is a non-diegetic, environmental tell. [Designer report] — [Iwata Asks: Punch-Out!!](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/punchout/0/3)
- Left 4 Dead: the Tank's "Music change, Tank's yells and heavy footfalls create powerful moment of Dramatic Anticipation". Boomer vomit tells you "the mob is coming to get you, soon…". [Designer report] — [Booth, GDC 2009 L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Fighting-game frame data: every move has startup (before it can hit, and you can be hit), active frames and recovery (you can be hit until it ends). Long recovery makes a move punishable, especially when it whiffs ("whiff punish"). Moves trade startup speed against recovery safety. [Lore/community reference] — [SRK wiki: Frame Data](https://srk.shib.live/w/Frame_Data); [DashFight: Frame Data](https://dashfight.com/news/fighting-games-essentials-frame-data-3156) [Secondary: search summary]
- Rabin: use the animation principles of anticipation and ease-in/ease-out. "Discontinuous movement is incredibly unnatural and draws attention to the inauthenticity." Intelligent creatures "stop, they reflect, they hesitate... they size up their opponent", and "enemies that temporarily back off are much more enjoyable adversaries" (citing Pac-Man's attack/retreat waves). [Lore/Designer report] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)
- Shmup/bullet-hell design (Boghog):
  - "Aimed" patterns are "Good for pressure, allows conscious manipulation by the player". Static patterns are good obstacles. Random patterns keep things fresh but "can create unfair situations".
  - "Single stray bullets are hard to read and can often feel unfair." Unusual trajectories may need trails. Bullets use bright cores with dark borders.
  - Generous player-shot hitboxes and enemy hurtboxes compensate for small aiming errors.
  - [Designer report/Lore] — [Boghog's bullet hell shmup 101](https://shmups.wiki/library/Boghog's_bullet_hell_shmup_101)
- **What happens when enemies react instantly or re-decide freely (player perception):** Elden Ring players complain that enemies "input read" and react on the first frame of a heal or cast. They also complain about delayed attacks, and bosses that seem to speed up if you roll early and slow down if you roll late, which shifts the lesson from "I didn't react properly" to "I haven't learned the arbitrary timing yet." [Community; Secondary search summary] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3316358999125136735); [AnimeClick on input reading](https://www.animeclick.it/news/96022-elden-ring-i-nemici-leggono-gli-input-alcuni-piu-o-meno-si)
- Counter-example where instant re-decision was chosen on purpose: TLOU's OpportunisticShooter drops whatever it is doing, mid-animation, to shoot when it has a line, because slow transitions let rushers go untouched. Only one NPC holds that role at a time. [Designer report] — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Assessment of the working hypothesis ("every maneuver has a visible tell and a counter; commit once started").**
  - *Supported for high-damage attacks.* Every source above ties fairness to anticipation plus a learnable response, and frame-data culture ties depth to commitment and recovery.
  - *Too strong as stated for every maneuver.* Halo explicitly wanted "Unpredictable" alongside "Intelligible". Bullet-hell design treats aimed, visible streams as self-telegraphing. TLOU deliberately lets one shooter react instantly.
  - A better-supported version: **tells scale with lethality.** Big-damage commitments (railgun release, missile launch, ram/boost attack) get explicit tells and recovery windows. Low-damage pressure (laser streams, autocannon) can be untelegraphed, because the projectiles themselves are the readable signal. Movement can change freely, but only through physically visible means (thrust, yaw).
- **Commitment and the twitch complaint:** Rabin's point that discontinuous movement reads as fake, and the player hatred of first-frame input reading, both argue for **commitment windows on decisions**: a minimum hold time once a maneuver starts, and reaction delays before responding to player inputs. This is the most direct structural fix for "pilots twitch".
- **Ship-duel translation of tells (no animation needed):**
  - Railgun: a charge glow on the barrel plus a rising audio whine plus the nose locking onto the player. This is the classic wind-up, and the charge time is the commitment window.
  - Charge laser: the same pattern with a shorter charge.
  - Boost: an engine-flare or exhaust-color pulse a fraction of a second before the impulse. The cooldown afterward is a natural punish window (show it as a dimmed engine).
  - Missiles: a lock-on tone and indicator (genre-standard).
  - Grenades dropped backward: the tell is the pursued enemy throttling down or straightening out in front of the player; the counter is to break off the direct chase line.
  - Lasers with heat: a visible heat glow or vent plume makes an "overheated" punish window.
- **Counters must exist in the movement model.** Strafe (decoupled facing) gives a perpendicular dodge against charged hitscan. Asteroids give line-of-sight breaks. Boost gives a burst escape. A railgun with charge time and no lead against a strafing target already has a counter. Instant hitscan with zero charge and perfect aim has none, which fits the reported "die quickly when it gets a shot".

### Gaps
- No primary FromSoftware statement on telegraph design philosophy was found. The Dark Souls/Elden Ring material is community perception only.
- I found no primary source stating what happens to perceived fairness when enemies can cancel attacks freely. The fighting-game restriction of cancels is community knowledge, not a cited design rationale.
- No controlled study measuring the effect of telegraph presence or duration on perceived fairness or intelligence.

## 5. Believable mistakes and handicaps: designed error, limited perception, reaction delay, rubber-banding, aim models, pacing directors

### Takeaway
Designers agree on a hierarchy. Handicaps that look like human limitation (reaction delay, aim that settles over time, limited perception, visibly near-missing shots, skill-based slowdowns) feel fair. Handicaps that break the shared rules (extra power or speed for the AI, perfect knowledge, reacting to inputs instead of outcomes) feel like cheating when noticed. The most concrete, code-level aim model for space fighters is FreeSpace 2's: lead and accuracy improve the longer the target stays in range. Pacing directors (Left 4 Dead) adjust threat *population* based on player stress rather than adjusting individual enemy skill. Evidence: almost entirely designer report. Rabin's reaction-time numbers are drawn from cognitive-psychology literature.

### Cited Findings
- **Reaction time** (Rabin, Game AI Pro 2): use roughly 0.2 s for simple reaction (visual mean 0.19–0.22 s) and roughly 0.4 s for recognition / go-no-go (0.38 s). Add time for weak stimuli (far away), aiming after detection, more complex choices and attention lapses. [Designer report citing psych literature] — [Rabin, "Agent Reaction Time"](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter05_Agent_Reaction_Time_How_Fast_Should_An_AI_React.pdf). Also: "Humans are incapable of instantaneous reaction... Use these times as the baseline to always delay the results of a decision." — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)
- **Settling aim, FreeSpace 2 (open-source code):**
  - When computing where to shoot, the AI scales its lead by how long the target has been in range. Before `range_time` (2 s base, plus a per-skill `ai_in_range_time` added only when the shooter is hostile to the player's team), the lead is only partial and grows with time in range.
  - After that it leads fully but adds a random error vector of size `(1 − accuracy) × 4 × (1 + 4 × (1 − t / (2 × range_time)))`, shrinking to `(1 − accuracy) × 4` once the target has been in range for `2 × range_time`.
  - Other per-skill knobs include `$Predict Position Delay`, `$Max Aim Update Delay`, `$AI Turn Time Scale`, `$Hostile AI Fire Delay Scale`, `$Player Damage Factor`, `$AI Countermeasure Firing Chance`, `$Chance AI Has to Fire Missiles at Player`, `$Glide Attack Percent`, `$Circle Strafe Percent`, `$Random Sidethrust Percent`, `$Stalemate Time Threshold` and `$Stalemate Distance Threshold`.
  - [Primary source code] — [FS2 Open aicode.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp); [FS2 Open ai_profiles.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai_profiles.cpp)
- **Visible deliberate misses** (Ocio Barriales): only token holders hit. Misses are aimed where the player will *see* them: tracers whizzing past the face at eye level, the top of half-cover, the ground at the peek-out side, destructibles in the player's field of view. 3D-positioned sound keeps the sense of being under fire. [Designer report] — [Ocio Barriales, Game AI Pro 3 ch.33](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter33_Using_Your_Combat_AI_Accuracy_to_Balance_Difficulty.pdf)
- **Accuracy keyed to the player's desired behavior** (DOOM 2016): accuracy is reduced when the player moves at top speed and rises as they slow, which rewards the intended push-forward style. [Designer report, Secondary] — [Game Developer: AI of DOOM 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)
- **Designed error philosophy:** Mick West (Neversoft): "The computer has to throw the game in order to make it fun." For weak AI, "an intelligent mistake consists of figuring out what you should do, and then not doing it, so long as not doing it does not make you look stupid." Players must feel the opponent "was really trying to beat them." [Designer report] — [West, Game Developer 2009](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/bonus-feature-intelligent-mistakes-key-to-believable-ai)
- Lidén (2004): balance AI "intelligence" against the player's ability to win by tuning "artificial stupidity" so the AI loses "without looking unintelligent". Secondary summaries attribute "miss the first time" (to show the direction of attack without hurting the player), "warn the player" (e.g. shout before attacking) and "kung-fu style" fighting to him. [Lore, Secondary; chapter itself not retrieved] — [Wikipedia: Artificial stupidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_stupidity)
- **Racing rubber-banding** (Nic Melder, Game AI Pro): when done poorly it "can leave the player feeling cheated, especially if the AI, racing in the same car as they, obviously has more speed." "It is better to modify the driver skill... as the drivers are still in the same cars and so no 'cheating' is happening." Power changes are a last resort beyond the skill range. [Designer report] — [Melder, Game AI Pro ch.42](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter42_A_Rubber-Banding_System_for_Gameplay_and_Race_Management.pdf)
- **Racing skill and mistake parameters** (Tomlinson & Melder):
  - Skill is the primary characteristic, applied to grip in cornering and braking calculations. Secondary characteristics are aggression (follows closer, needs less overtaking space), control (e.g. "may take twice as long to push the throttle in") and mistakes ("random errors in corner speed calculation").
  - The usual rubber-banding goal is "beating the player at the start of the race, but... losing at the finish".
  - AIs may query each other's intentions ("Are you about to turn left?"), with fuzziness added because real drivers read signs imperfectly.
  - [Designer report] — [Tomlinson & Melder, Game AI Pro ch.38](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter38_An_Architecture_Overview_for_AI_in_Racing_Games.pdf)
- **Left 4 Dead AI Director** (Michael Booth, GDC 2009):
  - Each survivor's "emotional intensity" is a float. It rises with damage taken, incapacitation, being pulled off ledges, and nearby infected deaths (inversely with distance). It decays over time, but not while infected are actively engaging.
  - Population states: Build Up (full threat population until intensity crosses a peak threshold), Sustain Peak (3–5 s), Peak Fade (minimal population until intensity decays and a natural break occurs), Relax (minimal population for 30–45 s or until survivors travel far enough). Boss encounters are not affected.
  - The design also forbids "invisible leashes, teleporting stragglers closer, dealing 'out of bounds' damage", because "players rebel against overtly heavy-handed punishments."
  - [Designer report] — [Booth, GDC 2009 L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- **Perception cheating, measured by what players notice** (TLOU): "the NPCs did not cheat with regard to knowing the player's location in most circumstances". They did cheat to shorten the search phase: if the player moved >5 m from where NPCs believed, an NPC was sent to approach. This cut a roughly 2-minute combat cycle to about 30 s after focus tests found the long version unresponsive. [Designer report] — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- **Player prediction must be toned down:** N-gram prediction of player moves works so well in Mortal Kombat-style fighting games "that they have to be toned down in order to give the player a chance to win" (citing Millington 2006). [Designer report] — [Vasquez, Game AI Pro ch.48](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter48_Implementing_N-Grams_for_Player_Prediction_Proceedural_Generation_and_Stylized_AI.pdf)
- **Pac-Man breather:** ghosts "slow down again to give the player a breather if Pac-Man dies" (Iwatani, GDC 2011 postmortem as reported). Attacks "were designed to come in waves... as opposed to an endless assault." [Designer report, Secondary] — [Nintendo World Report: Iwatani GDC 2011](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/feature/25579/gdc-2011-toru-iwatani-pac-man-postmortem); [Pac-Man Dossier](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pac-man-dossier)
- **Superhuman racing AI and sportsmanship** (Gran Turismo Sophy, Sony AI): the team found "just how thin the line is between an agent that wins at all costs and one that races competitively without violating racing etiquette." The stated aim for deployment was "an achievable, fun racing experience, whether they were a beginner or an advanced racer". No player-response metrics were published in the retrospective. [Designer report] — [Sony AI: GT Sophy five years on](https://ai.sony/blog/gran-turismo-sophy-five-years-on-from-nature-cover-to-open-frontier)

### Inferences
- Rough fairness ranking implied by these sources (inference, not measured):
  1. Reaction delay and limited perception (human-like, invisible as handicaps).
  2. Settling aim, deliberately visible near-misses, and skill-parameter slowdowns.
  3. Damage-pacing tokens and population pacing (invisible when done well).
  4. Stat or physics boosts and perfect information. These read as cheating once noticed, especially when the AI flies "the same car" (the same ship) as the player.
- **Ship-duel translation (most important for the current complaints).** "Perfect aim, no handicap, player dies fast" is the clearest mismatch with the literature. Copy FS2's model nearly verbatim:
  - Aim error and lead quality improve with continuous time-on-target and degrade when the target jinks (reset or partially reset the timer on large lateral acceleration).
  - A per-pilot skill value sets the floor error and the settle time.
  - Lead prediction uses a delayed sample of player velocity (Predict Position Delay), so a sharp strafe or boost "fools" the shot. This also creates the "counter" in the working hypothesis.
- **Reaction delay:** gate every AI response to a player event (boost, direction reversal, entering line of fire, missile launch) behind 0.2–0.4 s, plus skill-dependent extra. That alone removes "it read my inputs" moments and reduces twitching, because the AI isn't re-planning on every frame of input.
- **Designed mistakes that read as human in a ship duel:** overshooting a turn when chasing, an occasional mistimed boost into an asteroid, overheating the lasers by holding fire too long (the heat mechanic makes this a natural, readable error), firing a railgun slightly early. Race AI's "mistake characteristic" (random error in a key calculation) is the template: perturb the pilot's estimate, never its physics.
- **Director-style pacing for one to three enemies:** L4D-style intensity (player damage taken, near-misses, time under lock) can drive an aggression budget, e.g. after the player takes a big hit, pilots shift to repositioning for a few seconds. This is the Pac-Man "breather" and Griesemer's "pushback" in one mechanism, and it is invisible if expressed as pilots choosing a reposition maneuver rather than freezing.

### Gaps
- No controlled study comparing player perception of different handicap types (reaction delay vs aim error vs damage pacing) was found.
- Hunicke's DDA work: I retrieved only the 2004 Hamlet design paper ([Hunicke & Chapman](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/Hamlet.pdf)), which proposes but does not report an evaluation. The often-cited claim that players didn't notice DDA adjustments (Hunicke 2005, ACE) was not verified.
- No primary source retrieved for commercial FPS bot aim models (Counter-Strike, Halo, Call of Duty). FS2 is the only code-level example here.

## 6. Empirical and academic work on perceived competence, believability and enjoyment

### Takeaway
The measured findings point the same way but are thin. Players enjoy opponents that beat them sometimes but not always (Yannakakis & Hallam, 30 subjects, Pac-Man), and near-optimal opponents score low on that metric. Variety of opponent behavior across games adds interest. Communicating state via dialogue can substitute for behavioral complexity (Al Enezi & Verbrugge 2023). Coherence and consistency of NPC behavior shapes perceived intelligence (Poivet et al. 2025). "Human-like" and "skilled" are different axes: in BotPrize 2012, human players were rated less human than the best bots. Nothing I found directly measures "fun to fight" for skilled one-on-one AI opponents in action games.

### Cited Findings
- Yannakakis & Hallam, "Capturing Player Enjoyment in Computer Games":
  - Predator/prey interest is defined by three criteria. (1) "neither too hard nor too easy", i.e. opponents "kill the prey (player) sometimes but not always... highly effective opponent behaviors are not interesting behaviors". (2) Diversity in opponent behavior between games. (3) Opponents' spatial diversity. Both easy-killing and near-optimal ("too hard") opponents receive low challenge scores.
  - Validated with 30 subjects playing pairwise comparisons of 5 Pac-Man ghost controllers. The interest metric I correlated with human preference at r = 0.4444 (p = 1.17×10⁻⁸). An evolved neural model reached r = 0.5432 (p = 3.89×10⁻¹²).
  - Neither player type nor order significantly affected judgments. With appropriate challenge, entertainment was high even when behavioral diversity ("curiosity") was low.
  - [Measured] — [Yannakakis & Hallam, "Capturing Player Enjoyment"](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22896/1/Capturing_Player_Enjoyment_in_Computer_Games.pdf)
  - Note: a tool summary of this paper said "17 subjects". The paper text says 30, and I have used 30.
- The same paper notes that the field's "implicit hypothesis... that intelligent opponent behaviors enable the player to gain more satisfaction" had no supporting evidence, since enjoyment was rarely defined or measured. [Measured/meta] — [Yannakakis & Hallam](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22896/1/Capturing_Player_Enjoyment_in_Computer_Games.pdf)
- Al Enezi & Verbrugge (AIIDE 2023): contextual dialogue made simple guard behavior match complex behavior in enjoyment (see Q2). [Measured] — [AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27512)
- Poivet, Pelachaud & Auvray (arXiv 2025): two experiments in a military shooter. Coherent and consistent NPC design reinforces expectations, incoherent design challenges them, and this shifts ratings of perceived intelligence and believability. [Measured] — [arXiv 2512.07388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07388v1)
- 2K BotPrize (Unreal Tournament 2004, 2008–2012): judges rated opponents' humanness during deathmatch, with chat disabled. In 2012, UT^2 (UT Austin; neuroevolution plus replaying observed human behavior) and MirrorBot (mimicked opponents' reactions) each scored 52% humanness. Human players averaged about 40% (UT news release) or 41.4% (Wikipedia). [Measured] — [Wikipedia: Computer game bot Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_game_bot_Turing_test); [UT Austin news, 2012](https://news.utexas.edu/2012/09/26/artificially-intelligent-game-bots-pass-the-turing-test-on-turings-centenary/)
- Halo's two-condition survey (Q1) is the only industry datapoint linking toughness and lifespan to perceived intelligence. [Measured, undocumented method] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Rabin's expectation-management argument (anthropomorphism research, the wine-price experiment) is cited psychology, not a games study. Rabin uses it to argue that marketing and loading-screen tips that highlight the AI raise perceived quality. [Lore built on adjacent science] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)

### Inferences
- What best correlates with "fun to fight" in this thin literature:
  1. Win/loss uncertainty near the middle (not optimal play).
  2. Variety of behavior across encounters.
  3. Legibility of the AI's state and intent.
  4. Consistency of that state with the AI's presentation.
  Raw competence is not on the list.
- BotPrize implies the developer's "convincingly competent" goal needs readable purpose, not human imitation for its own sake. Judges often misread real humans as bots, so humanlike noise does not itself read as skill.
- **Ship-duel translation:** the Yannakakis criterion "kills the player sometimes but not always" lines up with the developer's skill-range goal. The current perfect-aim pilots that kill fast when they connect are at the near-optimal end, which the Pac-Man data scores as low interest. Playtesting remains the right instrument. Two cheap logged proxies are consistent with the literature: variance in time-to-kill across fights (behavior diversity) and the player win rate per pilot tier.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures perceived intelligence or enjoyment for 1v1 or 1v3 skilled-opponent action combat (fighting games, dogfights, duels).
- No study isolating predictable-vs-unpredictable opponent policy with fixed difficulty in an action game.
- Flow-theory (Csikszentmihalyi/Chen) and DDA user studies were not retrieved in primary form.

## 7. Translating to vehicles: telegraphing intent without body animation

### Takeaway
Vehicle and ship games swap body animation for four channels: (1) **motion itself** (nose direction, thrust, characteristic maneuvers), (2) **shared visible resources** (heat/flux bars, charge glows, lock tones), (3) **radio and audio**, and (4) **HUD and non-diegetic indicators** (lock warnings, off-screen arrows, named-ace callouts). Sourced examples here are FreeSpace 2's AI code, Starsector's flux-driven back-off (community-documented), Ace Combat's named aces, God of War / Spider-Man off-screen rules, Punch-Out's audience flash, and bullet-hell readability rules. Evidence: designer report and community documentation. No studies.

### Cited Findings
- FreeSpace 2 exposes movement signatures as AI profile parameters: `$Glide Attack Percent`, `$Circle Strafe Percent`, `$Glide Strafe Percent`, `$Random Sidethrust Percent`. It also has threat caps like `$Max Missiles Locked on Player` and `$Max Player Attackers`, and per-skill countermeasure chance. [Primary source code] — [FS2 Open ai_profiles.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai_profiles.cpp)
- Starsector (community wiki on observed AI behavior):
  - Ships back off at a "critical flux threshold" of about 80–85% and "refuse to engage enemies until it reduces flux to below somewhere around 10–15%". The BACKING_OFF and DO_NOT_PURSUE AI flags cover this.
  - Officer personalities change engagement range and risk. Timid: "Likes to stay back, has very little initiative to attack". Cautious: longest weapon range. Steady: default battle line. Aggressive: shortest weapon range, "strongly considers target's flux levels for moving closer". Reckless: "Throws all restrictions out of the window".
  - The AI "tends to 'switch' personalities based on how many friendlies and enemies are present".
  - [Community documentation] — [Starsector wiki: AI Behaviour](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/AI_Behaviour)
- Ace Combat: since Ace Combat 2, "enemy aces" (named aircraft) are a series staple, usually with special paint schemes. [Community wiki] — [Ace Combat wiki: AC2 Aces](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/Ace_Combat_2/Aces). NPC countermeasures first appeared in limited quantities in Ace Combat 5, with some named aces among the first to use them, so skill was expressed through a visible defensive behavior. [Community] — [Skyward FM: Countermeasures in Ace Combat](https://www.skywardfm.com/posts/hardpoint:-countermeasures-in-ace-combat)
- Star Wars: Squadrons reviews note human multiplayer opponents are "much more alive and harder to hit" than the campaign's AI fighters, a common perception gap for space-combat AI. [Review, Secondary search summary] — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/star-wars-squadrons-review/)
- Non-diegetic tells are accepted when consistent: Punch-Out's audience flash before Bald Bull's charge ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/punchout/0/3)); God of War's red/white off-screen arrows ([Sheth GDC digest](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)); Spider-Man's longer off-screen fire delays feeding the Spidey-Sense indicator ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/designing-ai-to-do-anything-a-spider-can-in-i-marvel-s-spider-man-i-)). [Designer report]
- Left 4 Dead uses distinctive audio and music cues per boss and special as "Dramatic Anticipation" (Tank music, yells, footfalls). [Designer report] — [Booth, GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Shmup readability: high-contrast projectiles (bright core, dark border), grouping bullets into lines, trails for odd trajectories, and aimed streams that the player can deliberately manipulate. [Designer report] — [Boghog's bullet hell shmup 101](https://shmups.wiki/library/Boghog's_bullet_hell_shmup_101)
- Charge-up projectile telegraph (particles, rising sound, flash) as a general pattern. [Designer report] — [Stout](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing)
- Rabin: movement speed conveys emotion. "Fast movements convey being upset, agitated, confused, and nervous. Slow movements convey being relaxed, calm, and in control." [Lore] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The Starsector pattern is the best fit for this game.** The AI obeys the *same visible resource* as the player (flux there, laser heat here), so its state is legible from shared UI. Its back-off is a predictable, punishable window, and its personalities are just thresholds and ranges. Displaying enemy heat (a hull glow or small bar) and having pilots disengage at a personality-specific heat level gives readable state, a punish window, believable "mistakes" (reckless pilots overheat) and distinct personalities from one mechanic.
- **Motion as body language:** with decoupled facing, the most expressive channel is *where the nose points versus where the ship drifts*. Calm, skilled pilots: smooth yaw, small corrections, holding the nose on target while strafing. Nervous or novice pilots: overcorrecting and oscillating yaw (a deliberate version of the current twitch, used as a skill signature rather than a bug). Rabin's fast = agitated, slow = in control mapping applies directly.
- **Off-screen threat in top-down:** edge arrows colored by intent (idle, maneuvering, winding up a railgun or holding a lock) plus slower wind-ups off-screen are the Spider-Man / God of War solution and translate one-to-one.
- **Radio chatter** (Q2) is the vehicle-genre answer to missing faces and voices, and is native to the touchstones.
- **Silhouette and livery:** Ace Combat-style named aces with unique paint schemes are the minimal identity marker when everyone flies the same hull.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved or verified: FTL (enemy weapon-charge visibility), Elite Dangerous (NPC combat rank behavior), Armored Core (boss telegraphs), Star Wars: Squadrons AI design talk (Ian Frazier's GDC 2022 postmortem exists but wasn't accessed), and Ace Combat developer statements on radio chatter or ace design. These are frequently cited examples but are unsourced in these notes.
- Starsector evidence is a community wiki, not developer documentation.

## 8. Distinct enemy personalities: recognizable and memorable opponents

### Takeaway
The minimum that makes opponents read as distinct, per the cleanest example (Pac-Man), is **one different decision rule per enemy plus a distinct visual identity**. Each ghost differs mainly by target point (and Clyde by a distance rule), color-coded. Richer systems add (a) parameter-level profiles (racing skill, aggression, control and mistake values; Starsector engagement range and flux tolerance), (b) role or archetype rules (Halo races, L4D specials), (c) memory of the player and remarks about past encounters (Nemesis), and (d) names, liveries and introductions (Ace Combat aces). Evidence: designer report. The Pac-Man ghosts are the strongest "it works" case through longevity, not measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Pac-Man (Iwatani, GDC 2011 as reported): the ghosts "do not have actual AI; they simply follow an algorithm to give the impression that each has a mind of its own." Iwatani's manager wanted all four ghosts red, and a 50-vote poll changed that to four colors with separate algorithms. [Designer report, Secondary] — [NWR: Iwatani GDC 2011](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/feature/25579/gdc-2011-toru-iwatani-pac-man-postmortem)
- Actual chase targeting (reverse-engineered, Pac-Man Dossier):
  - Blinky targets Pac-Man's tile. Pinky targets 4 tiles ahead (with an overflow bug when facing up). Inky doubles the vector from Blinky to a point 2 tiles ahead of Pac-Man. Clyde targets Pac-Man when more than 8 tiles away, otherwise retreats to his scatter corner.
  - Level 1 alternates scatter 7 s / chase 20 s / scatter 7 s / chase 20 s / scatter 5 s / chase indefinitely. Blinky becomes "Cruise Elroy" (faster) when 20 dots remain on level 1.
  - [Measured from code] — [The Pac-Man Dossier](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pac-man-dossier)
  - **Conflict:** Iwatani's talk as reported describes Inky as mirroring and Clyde as random, a simplification that contradicts the Dossier's code-level rules. Treat the Dossier as authoritative for mechanics.
- Racing driver profiles: skill (primary) plus aggression, control and mistakes (secondary). "Support these traits with driver profiles viewable pre-game or perhaps in-game signals like audio commentary." [Designer report] — [Tomlinson & Melder, Game AI Pro ch.38](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter38_An_Architecture_Overview_for_AI_in_Racing_Games.pdf)
- Halo racial personality via simple rules (Grunts flee easily, Elites take cover when hurt, Jackals use shields) and "Clear racial roles". [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Left 4 Dead: each Special Infected "exists to address specific gameplay issues". The Hunter punishes lone wolves, the Smoker pulls apart tight teams, the Boomer breaks "shoot everything that moves". [Designer report] — [Booth, GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Nemesis system (Chris Hoge, Monolith, "Helping Players Hate (or Love) Their Nemesis", GDC 2018). Per secondary summaries, captains remember previous encounters (whether they killed you, you killed them, you exploited their weakness, or you fled) and issue matching insults and threats. [Designer report, Secondary] — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025150/Helping-Players-Hate-(or-Love)); [de Plater Q&A, God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/2014/06/middle-earth-shadow-mordor-michael-de-plater)
- Starsector personalities as parameter bundles (engagement range, flux tolerance, fighter allocation). [Community documentation] — [Starsector wiki](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/AI_Behaviour)
- Rabin: "a strong personality goes a long way to covering up any inconsistencies in the behavior or logic of a character. Strong personalities can be irrational and unpredictable, allowing incredible leeway." [Lore] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)
- Ace Combat named aces with special paint schemes. [Community wiki] — [Ace Combat wiki](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/Ace_Combat_2/Aces)

### Inferences
- **Minimum viable distinction for two same-hull pilots (inference):**
  1. A livery or color plus a name/callsign.
  2. One different *visible* decision rule, e.g. preferred engagement range (railgun sniper at long range vs autocannon brawler that closes), or preferred attack geometry (Pinky-like cutoff that aims at where the player will be vs Blinky-like tailer).
  3. One signature tell or maneuver (always boost-reverses after a pass; always drops grenades when tailed).
  Pac-Man shows (2) alone, with color, is enough to read as personality.
- **Skill range via parameters:** use FS2-style accuracy and settle time, reaction delay, prediction delay, heat discipline (the Starsector-like back-off threshold) and mistake rate as the skill axis. Use range, aggression and maneuver preferences as the personality axis. Keeping the two axes separate gives a matrix of distinct pilots with few knobs, matching racing-AI practice (skill primary, style secondary).
- **Rabin's point about personality covering inconsistency** matters for a roguelike: a pilot introduced as "reckless" can make bad decisions that read as character rather than bugs.
- **Nemesis-lite for a roguelike:** a recurring named ace who remembers the last fight and comments on it in a radio line (if the player retreated, beat them, or used grenades) is a cheap high-impact version. It fits the Scar touchstone, a single recurring ace with a reputation.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence on how many parameters or rules must differ before players perceive two opponents as distinct individuals.
- Nemesis-system specifics (trait counts, playtest data) were not retrieved from the primary talk.

## 9. Failure modes: when readable became predictable and exploitable, and how designers balanced it

### Takeaway
The documented balancing moves are:
- getting unpredictability from **reaction to context**, not randomness (Halo);
- **varying the context** around a readable core loop (Halo's "no 30-second stretch repeated");
- **structured unpredictability** of *when and where* threats occur, while *what* stays legible (L4D);
- **escalating complexity** of tells across the game (Monster Hunter);
- **multiple variants of a reaction while keeping its type consistent** (Ocio Barriales);
- **damping prediction** so the AI doesn't out-read the player (N-grams).

The failures run both ways. Too predictable means memorized and boring (L4D on scripted triggers, and repetition-spotting). Too clever means unreadable and cheap (Elden Ring complaints, superhuman prediction, GT Sophy's unsporting early behavior). The most relevant failure for the current complaints is TLOU's frame-to-frame instability of flank choices, fixed by changing to a stable cost shape. Evidence: designer report and community perception.

### Cited Findings
- Halo: "Discarded: Randomness". Unpredictability should come from reactive AI and analog reactions to position and timing. "Things to Avoid: Subtlety, Looking Broken, Insufficient Challenge." [Designer report] — [Halo GDC 2002 slides](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)
- Griesemer: the 30-second loop is constant but "No 30 second stretch of Halo is ever repeated; the missions are constantly changing the context on you" (new environments, weapons, vehicles, enemy mixes). [Designer report] — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html)
- Left 4 Dead: avoid manually placed scripts with respect to "when" and "where" ("what" can be ok). Scripts kill replayability because "Players learn all script locations quickly" and they remove suspense. The answer was "Layers of Structured Unpredictability", e.g. 75% of mobs come from behind and boss events are shuffled Tank/Witch/Nothing with no successive repeats. [Designer report] — [Booth, GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Ocio Barriales: players should understand the systems and form expectations, "But this also means our brains will easily spot repetition". Have "multiple versions of the same reaction, but the type of reaction" stays consistent. [Designer report] — [Ocio Barriales 2021](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIProOnlineEdition2021/GameAIProOnlineEdition2021_Chapter11_You_had_me_at_AAAAHHH_On_the_importance_of_reactions_in_game_AI.pdf)
- Yannakakis & Hallam: behavior diversity between games is one of three measured interest criteria (Q6). [Measured] — [Yannakakis & Hallam](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22896/1/Capturing_Player_Enjoyment_in_Computer_Games.pdf)
- Monster Hunter: simple, easy tells early, more complex monsters later, so readability is taught and then escalated. [Designer report] — [Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/the-developers-of-monster-hunter-explain-what-its-like-to-build-monsters)
- Bullet-hell: aimed patterns are *meant* to be manipulable by the player (readable is the skill). Randomness "can create unfair situations". [Designer report] — [Boghog](https://shmups.wiki/library/Boghog's_bullet_hell_shmup_101)
- Over-reading the player: N-gram prediction in fighting games must be toned down to give the player a chance. [Designer report] — [Vasquez, Game AI Pro ch.48](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter48_Implementing_N-Grams_for_Player_Prediction_Proceedural_Generation_and_Stylized_AI.pdf)
- Anti-exploit counters that read as cheap: Elden Ring's delayed attacks and input reading are widely perceived as designed to catch reactive rolling. Players say the lesson becomes memorizing "arbitrary timing". [Community, Secondary] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3316358999125136735)
- Superhuman-but-unsporting: shortly before the July 2021 exhibition, GT Sophy had become "ruthlessly effective at contact and obstruction". It lost the first head-to-head event, then won after reward retuning for etiquette. [Designer report, Secondary] — [Sony AI blog](https://ai.sony/blog/gran-turismo-sophy-five-years-on-from-nature-cover-to-open-frontier); search summaries of press coverage, e.g. [NPR/Michigan Public 2022](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2022-02-23/a-i-has-mastered-gran-turismo-and-one-autonomous-car-designer-is-taking-note)
- **Instability (twitch) failure:** TLOU first chose flank routes from an exposure map, but "the flank routes could vary wildly from one frame to the next" as the player moved. They replaced it with a fixed cost shape along the "combat vector" (the average of NPC positions weighted by recent shots), which produced stable flanks that went around "precisely [where] the player expected". [Designer report] — [McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- Token systems fail when waiting becomes visible (Q3). [Designer report] — [Ocio Barriales 2016](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter33_Using_Your_Combat_AI_Accuracy_to_Balance_Difficulty.pdf)
- Relentlessness fails: "your AI should stop pursuing the player relentlessly, similar to a terminator... enemies that temporarily back off are much more enjoyable adversaries." Also "Characters with nothing to do are a clear signal that the AI is fake". [Lore] — [Rabin, Game AI Pro 3 ch.1](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter01_The_Illusion_of_Intelligence.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Mapping the developer's complaints to documented failure modes and fixes (inference):**
  - *Twitch* = frame-to-frame re-decision on noisy inputs (TLOU flank instability; Rabin's discontinuous movement). Fixes: commitment windows, hysteresis, and stable cost shapes relative to a smoothed "combat vector".
  - *Timid* = Halo's "insufficient challenge" and the token "waiting" failure. Fix: always have one pilot whose role is to press (TLOU OpportunisticShooter, with a damage-pacing token).
  - *Can't hold aim* = no settle model. Fix: FS2 time-on-target aim.
  - *Can't anticipate* = no player model. Fixes: lead prediction with delay, plus light pattern learning (N-gram-style prediction of the player's favorite break direction), deliberately damped.
  - *Ignore each other* = no coordinator. Fix: a role coordinator or slots.
  - *Sit in range* = no purpose and no tempo. Fixes: Pac-Man/L4D attack-retreat waves, Starsector heat back-off cycles, FS2-style stalemate thresholds that force a new maneuver after N seconds at stable range.
- **Readability without exploitability, for a ship duel:** keep the *mapping* from tell to threat fixed (railgun glow always means a railgun shot after X seconds). Vary *when, from where and which pilot* (L4D's when/where vs what). Escalate tell complexity with pilot skill (Monster Hunter): novice pilots telegraph long, aces telegraph short and mix feints, but the cue type never lies.
- **Feints:** none of the sources endorse fake tells. Elden Ring's delays show the risk. If aces get feints, make the feint itself readable (a charge that visibly vents without firing) so it reads as cleverness, not cheating. This is speculative and should be playtested.

### Gaps
- No documented case retrieved of a designer quantifying when a readable pattern became an exploit (e.g. measured cheese rates).
- No primary source on Pac-Man pattern memorization or Punch-Out pattern-exploit balancing was retrieved, although both are commonly cited.
- AlphaDogfight (DARPA 2020, an AI beat an F-16 pilot 5–0 in simulation) surfaced in search, but tactic details and their relevance to "fair fun" weren't retrieved ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2020/alphadogfight-trial)).
