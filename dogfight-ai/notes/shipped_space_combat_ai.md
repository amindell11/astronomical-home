# Shipped space-combat and flight-combat enemy pilot AI: architectures, vocabularies, and what developers said worked or failed

Scope note: primary sources here are mostly source code (FreeSpace 2 Open, Endless Sky, Naev, Ur-Quan Masters), which I downloaded and read directly; line numbers refer to the files as of 2026-10. Several developer sites (fractalsoftworks.com, wiki.hard-light.net, community.elitedangerous.com, moddb, GDC schedule) blocked automated fetches (HTTP 402/403), so some Starsector, FreeSpace-wiki and Elite claims rest on secondary pages or search-result summaries; these are flagged. "Transfer" notes judge fit for a solo-dev 2.5D game with decoupled facing (thrust/strafe/yaw independent), drag, speed cap and boost.

## Q1. FreeSpace 2 (fs2_open): how is the fighter AI structured, what maneuvers does it have, how does it choose, how do AI classes change behavior?

### Takeaway
FreeSpace 2's dogfight AI is a two-level state machine: a mode (`AIM_CHASE`, `AIM_EVADE`, `AIM_GUARD`...) set by mission goals, and inside chase a small vocabulary of named submodes (attack, super-attack, evade-squiggle, evade-brake, get-behind, get-away, continuous-turn, avoid) picked by hand-written geometric rules (dot products to/from enemy, distance, time since last hit) plus dice rolls, each with a minimum/maximum duration. Skill is mostly expressed through per-skill-level numeric arrays (accuracy, evasion, courage, patience, turn-time scale, fire delay) and through how quickly aim converges while the target stays in the sights — not through different behaviors.

### Cited Findings
**Mode / submode structure**
- Top-level AI modes include `AIM_CHASE`, `AIM_EVADE`, `AIM_GET_BEHIND` (commented "Not actually implemented"), `AIM_STAY_NEAR`, `AIM_GUARD`, `AIM_AVOID`, `AIM_WAYPOINTS`, `AIM_SAFETY` ("Seek safety at battle periphery"), `AIM_EVADE_WEAPON`, `AIM_STRAFE` (attack big ship by strafing), `AIM_LUA` (generic Lua-based mode). — [fs2open ai.h](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai.h)
- Chase submodes (the dogfight maneuver vocabulary): `SM_CONTINUOUS_TURN`, `SM_ATTACK`, `SM_EVADE_SQUIGGLE`, `SM_EVADE_BRAKE`, `SM_EVADE`, `SM_SUPER_ATTACK`, `SM_AVOID`, `SM_GET_BEHIND`, `SM_GET_AWAY`, `SM_EVADE_WEAPON`, `SM_FLY_AWAY`, `SM_ATTACK_FOREVER` ("Engine destroyed, attack never evading"), stealth find/sweep, big-ship approach/circle/parallel, plus later additions `AIS_CHASE_GLIDEATTACK` ("Glide while pointing and shooting") and `AIS_CHASE_CIRCLESTRAFE`. Strafe-vs-capital submodes: attack, avoid, retreat1/2, position, glide-attack. — [fs2open ai.h](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai.h)
- `ai_chase()` dispatches the current submode, then runs a "Maybe choose a new submode" block. Selection is ordered `if/else` rules on `dot_to_enemy` (am I pointing at him), `dot_from_enemy` (is he pointing at me), distance, enemy speed and timers. Examples from `SM_ATTACK`: circle-strafe if within 300 m + target radius and the target is slow and a per-ship-seeded roll passes `ai_circle_strafe_percent`; `SM_SUPER_ATTACK` if near full strength, no hit on target for 3 s, <500 m and target not in front (dot<0.5); `SM_GET_AWAY` if no hit for 6 s, <500 m, dot<0.2 and `frand() < skill/NUM_SKILL_LEVELS`; `SM_EVADE_BRAKE` if a fast small enemy at 50–200 m is more on my tail than I am on his; `SM_GET_BEHIND` if I'm roughly facing him and he's side-on; if he's on my tail within 150 m (threshold shifted by `ai_courage*.002`) roll get-away vs evade-squiggle; otherwise after 2 s 50/50 continuous-turn vs evade. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9342–9414](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Each submode has explicit exit/commit conditions: evade-squiggle lasts until 5 s or >300 m; evade-brake exits on re-acquiring aim (dot>0.9) or after 4 s; get-behind exits after 2 s or when behind; get-away runs 2–5 s or until 200–500 m away; glide attack lasts "at least as long as it takes to turn around and fire for a couple seconds"; circle strafe breaks after 8 s, if target too far, or target too fast. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9416–9634](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- `SM_SUPER_ATTACK` close-in (<100 m, not aligned, >5 s in mode) rolls one of five outcomes (continuous turn, stay, get-away-or-evade weighted by get-away chance, or evade vs get-away biased by skill) with the comment "Less likely to GET_AWAY at lower skill levels." — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9478–9514](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Anti-passivity rule: "If a very long time since attacked, attack no matter what!" — after 6 s without an attack submode, force `SM_SUPER_ATTACK`. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9290](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Anti-circling: `SM_GET_AWAY` is commented "Used to get away from opponent to prevent endless circling." A later SCP stalemate detector ("SUSHI: Alternate stalemate dection method: if nobody has hit each other for a while... shake things up somehow") rolls each second against `ai_patience` and switches to glide-attack or get-away. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9245, ~L9299–9325](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Developer intent survives in comments: on `SM_EVADE`, "Modified by MK on 5/5/97 to keep trying to regain attack mode. It's what a human would do." On evade-squiggle: "Now, evade straight away to try to get far away. The squiggling should protect against laser fire." After get-away the AI is handed `time_enemy_in_range = 2.0f` with the comment "Cheat. Presumably if they were running away from you, they were monitoring you!" — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L8152–8157, ~L9447, ~L9559](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Evade-squiggle is a time-sliced jink: a target point offset along the ship's own right/up vectors, chosen from bits of mission time, full throttle. Get-away flies along the away-vector plus a 100 m random offset at double throttle with afterburner if fuel >30%+jitter. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L8157–8221](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Random sidethrust (an SCP addition, default 0) re-rolls every 2 s and only applies when recently hit (5 s) or within primary-weapon range. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9259–9286](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)

**How weapons use is tied to flight (aim model)**
- Lead "learns in": while `time_enemy_in_range < range_time` (2 s, plus `ai_in_range_time` when attacking the player's team), predicted position is extrapolated only by `time_enemy_in_range * dist/weapon_speed`, i.e. partial lead; after that it computes a real intercept from the gun position. Comment: "Make it take longer for enemies to get player's allies in range based on skill level." — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L7238–7280](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Aim error scale is `(1-ai_accuracy)*4` once tracked for >2×range_time, and up to 5× larger right after acquisition, decaying linearly as tracking time grows. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L7282–7291](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Fire gate: tracking time accumulates only when `dot_to_enemy > 0.95 - 0.5*radius/(radius+dist)`; primaries fire only when dot exceeds `max(0.5, 0.90 + accuracy/10 - radius/dist)` and the target is within a range that shrinks when the target is fast ("Less likely to fire if far away and moving."). — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9661–9699](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Against slow, large targets it aims at a random point scaled by size/speed rather than the center. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L8123–8131](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)

**AI classes, skill levels, difficulty**
- `ai_class` holds per-skill-level arrays (5 levels): accuracy, evasion, courage, patience, countermeasure chance, in-range time, ammo/energy link thresholds, predict-position delay, shield-manage delay, fire-delay scales (friendly/hostile, primary/secondary), turn-time scale, glide/circle-strafe/glide-strafe/random-sidethrust percents, stalemate time/distance thresholds, chance to use missiles on player, max aim update delay, afterburner use factor, shockwave evade chance, get-away chance, secondary range multiplier, and `ai_class_autoscale`. — [fs2open ai.h](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai.h)
- AI class index is an ordinal skill ladder: default get-away chance = `(class_index + skill_level)/(num_classes + NUM_SKILL_LEVELS)`. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L9386](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- `ai_evasion` is a straight percentage roll for whether evasive turning happens; `ai_patience` gates stalemate-breaking; `ai_courage` shifts the "he's on my tail" threshold. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L5593, ~L9309, ~L9385](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Default difficulty table (Very Easy → Insane): `$AI Turn Time Scale: 3, 2.2, 1.6, 1.3, 1`; `$Hostile AI Fire Delay Scale: 4, 2.5, 1.75, 1.25, 1`; `$AI In Range Time: 2, 1.4, 0.75, 0, -1`; `$Predict Position Delay: 2, 1.5, 1.333, 0.5, 0`; `$AI Countermeasure Firing Chance: 0.2 … 1.1`; `$Max Player Attackers: 2, 3, 4, 5, 99`; `$Max Missiles Locked on Player: 2, 3, 4, 7, 99`; player-side buffs `$Player Damage Factor: 0.25 … 1`, `$Player Weapon Recharge Scale: 10, 4, 2.5, 2, 1.5`, `$Player Shield Recharge Scale: 4 … 1`, `$Player Afterburner Recharge Scale: 5 … 1`. Glide attack, circle strafe, glide strafe, random sidethrust and stalemate threshold all default to 0. — [fs2open def_files/ai_profiles.tbl](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/def_files/ai_profiles.tbl)
- Coordination is implicit via target assignment: candidate distance is inflated by `(num_attacking + 2)/2` for small ships and targets at the attacker cap are skipped, which spreads fighters across targets; per-difficulty `max_attackers` caps the number on the player. — [fs2open aicode.cpp ~L2324–2371, ~L10409](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Players observed the basic geometry the AI exploits: pure pursuit against a turning target overshoots; lag pursuit or throttling down fixes it (player forum post, not dev). — [GOG FreeSpace forum](https://www.gog.com/forum/freespace_series/pure_lead_pursuit_overshoot_and_how_to_avoid_it_a_k_a_i_keep_running_into_past_m/page1)
- FreeSpace was praised at release for "competent AI wingmen" (secondary, encyclopedic). — [Wikipedia: Descent: FreeSpace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent:_FreeSpace_%E2%80%93_The_Great_War)

### Inferences
- The retail FS2 dogfight vocabulary is about eight submodes; the "fancier" ones (glide attack, circle strafe, random sidethrust, stalemate breaking) are SCP additions defaulting off, inferred from the "SUSHI" comment tags and zero defaults. So the AI widely remembered as good ran on a small vocabulary with commitment timers, a forced re-attack after 6 s, and an anti-circling escape.
- The single most transferable FS2 mechanism for this game's "cannot hold aim" and "believable mistakes" complaints is the tracking-time aim model: error and lead quality improve the longer the target stays in the cone, and reset when tracking breaks. That makes jinking pay off for the player, makes misses look human, and gives a skill knob with readable effect.
- FS2 difficulty is heavily asymmetric handicapping (AI turns 3x slower, fires 4x less often, player takes 25% damage at Very Easy) plus attacker caps. That transfers trivially, but contradicts "same ship and controls as player"; for this game the turn-time and fire-delay knobs map to per-pilot reaction/aim parameters instead.
- Transfer: high. Submode entry rules are written in heading/aspect terms (dot to/from enemy) that map directly to a planar game, and decoupled facing makes `SM_GET_BEHIND` / evade-brake cheaper to execute (strafe instead of bank). The 3D banking code is irrelevant. "Get-away to break a circle" is the standard answer to the player circling while firing inward.

### Gaps
- Retail `ai.tbl` AI class names and values (e.g. ladder of named classes) could not be read: wiki.hard-light.net returned 403 and the retail table is not in the fs2open repo.
- No Volition postmortem or interview about FS2 AI design was found; the in-code comments (MK = presumably Mike Kulas; not verified) are the only developer statements found.
- AI goals (SEXP `ai-chase`, `ai-guard` etc.) map onto the modes above, but I did not read `aigoals.cpp`.

## Q2. 3D cockpit and flight-combat games (X-Wing/TIE Fighter/XWA, Wing Commander, Squadrons, Rogue Squadron, Elite Dangerous, Star Citizen, Everspace, House of the Dying Sun, Rebel Galaxy Outlaw, Ace Combat, Project Wingman): what is publicly known?

### Takeaway
Public detail is thin for most commercial titles. The best documented are Wing Commander (target-fixated pilots; WC2 added target leading; aces' "thinking" reprinted in the official strategy guide), Star Citizen (named behaviors such as "punishidle", "strafer", "jouster", an "orbital strafe maneuver", weighted target selection, explicit work on "tactic commitment"), and Elite Dangerous (rank-based skill curve; the 2.1 overshoot). Repeated themes: named per-pilot behaviors, difficulty via skill curves, and "cheating" complaints when AI stats or maneuvers exceed the player's.

### Cited Findings
**Wing Commander I/II**
- The WC1 rolling demo shipped with `BRAINS.C`, "an early version of the pilot behavior source code"; leaked pages show "how the Kilrathi approach a target, maneuver / evade and retreat for home." The *Wing Commander I & II Ultimate Strategy Guide* "reprints exactly how each enemy and ace 'thinks'", with Ken Demarest and Siobhan Beeman discussing WC1 AI (pp. 248–249) and WC2 changes (pp. 263–264). — [WCNews: Wingleader code](https://www.wcnews.com/news/7448); [WCNews forum thread](https://wcnews.com/chatzone/threads/how-does-the-computer-control-wingmen.30381/)
- Per the strategy guide (as summarized by a forum member): ships have one designated enemy they shoot or evade and "ignore everything else, unless events (like insults) make them change target"; WC1 ships aim where the enemy currently is, WC2 ships lead their targets. — [WCNews forum thread](https://wcnews.com/chatzone/threads/how-does-the-computer-control-wingmen.30381/) (secondary summary of a primary print source)
- WC lore names maneuvers (Kickstop, Sit-'n'-Spin); an indie dev building a WC homage (Squadron: Mercenaries) says it implemented Kickstop, Fish Hook, Tight Loop, Hard Brake, Sit-'n'-Spin and tail maneuvers "referenced from original Wing Commander design documentation." — [Squadron: Mercenaries devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)

**Squadron: Mercenaries (indie, 2024 devlog — relevant as a recent solo-dev account)**
- Architecture: hierarchical state machine (Mission: Patrol/Destroy/Wingman → Operation: Wander/Approach/Engage → Task: steering/scanning), switching to a decision tree during dogfights that reassesses on health and position; data-driven maneuvers make it easy to add maneuvers and restrict them by pilot skill. The original demo "suffered from 'space jousting'"; adding the named maneuvers produced opponents doing "complex evasive maneuvers." — [Squadron: Mercenaries devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)

**X-Wing / TIE Fighter / X-Wing Alliance**
- XWA AI ranks run from Novice/Officer (easy) to Top Ace/Jedi (hard) (player discussion). — [Steam XWA discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/361670/discussions/0/1742229167198349639)
- XWVM (fan Unity remake of X-Wing) reports there are no stats or specs for the original ranks, so AI was approximated empirically; the team rewrote the AI from scratch to match "characteristics and quirks", and says 1:1 behavior is impossible because the original AI's behavior depended on framerate, DOS vs Windows, and mission load. The issue tracker includes titles like "Top ace TIE fighters too accurate", "AI Turn Rate", "Bomber AI Evasion" (issue bodies were login-walled). — [Digital Trends on XWVM](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/star-wars-fans-recreate-1993s-xwing/); [XWVM issue list](https://bitbucket.org/xwvmteam/xwvm-lfs/issues/14/top-ace-tie-fighters-too-accurate)

**Star Citizen / Squadron 42 (CIG monthly reports)**
- Named ship-combat behaviors: "punishidle" ("Instead of statically circling the target, the AI will perform more maneuvers, particularly when targeted"), and improved "tactic commitments for the 'strafer' and 'jouster' behaviors"; defend-area AI now searches for an in-range firing position instead of sitting still when the target leaves the zone. — [SC Monthly Report Aug 2025](https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/20753)
- Target selection uses designer-tunable weights and threat scores, number of allies already on a target, and damage inflicted. — [SQ42 Monthly Report Aug 2019](https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/17232)
- Sub-component targeting ("prioritize attacking the weapons causing the most damage") required adapting "the orbital strafe maneuver"; "chatty"/"quiet" personality traits gate combat barks. — [SC Monthly Report June 2021](https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/18223)
- Alpha 3.23: "AI ship pilots have been toughened with new behaviors, based from real PU combat via collecting data from experienced dogfighters, to give them more move sets allowing them to do strafe runs, new orbits, more intelligent maneuvers and distancing." — [DSOGaming on 3.23](https://dsogaming.com/?p=179491)

**Elite Dangerous**
- For 2.1 (Engineers, 2016) Frontier flattened the skill curve for low/mid-rank NPCs and raised it sharply for Elite rank; NPCs also received Engineer weapon mods, and a networking bug let NPC weapons merge stats (e.g. a rail gun with pulse-laser fire rate). Players complained of overly aggressive, overly strong NPCs; Frontier stripped most Engineer upgrades from NPCs while keeping the improved behavior. — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/elite-dangerous-patch-stops-ai-developing-wmds); [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/frontier-pulls-new-feature-after-elites-ai-hunts-players/) (both via search-result summaries; Frontier's own forum post returned 403)

**Ace Combat / Project Wingman**
- AC7's pre-release trailer showcased enemies using a "Post Stall Maneuver" to evade pursuers and counter-attack (Japanese press summary). — [Dengeki Online](https://dengekionline.com/elem/000/001/595/1595563)
- Player complaints (speculation): AC7 AI "never loses energy" and out-turns the player; the ace Mihaly's "insane maneuvering just makes the fight drag on." — [Steam AC7 discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/502500/discussions/0/1840188800799010810)
- Project Wingman: across four difficulties enemy AI "ranges from ambivalent to cutthroat aggression" (review). — [Tech-Gaming review](https://tech-gaming.com/project-wingman)

**Rebel Galaxy Outlaw / Everspace**
- RGO gives the player an "auto-pursuit" button that manages speed and heading to keep a target in view, so the player only aims and fires — an accessibility design, not enemy AI. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/rebel-galaxy-outlaw-a-sort-of-prequel-to-space-combat-sim-rebel-galaxy-out-in-2019)
- Everspace developer tips recommend strafing in a continuous spiral as one of the best tactics against fighters (via search summary). — [Xbox Wire: Everspace developer tips](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2016/11/22/everspace-developer-tips-strategies/)

### Inferences
- Star Citizen's vocabulary (strafer, jouster, orbit/orbital strafe, punish-idle) and its explicit "tactic commitment" work look like the authored-maneuver-layer hypothesis in production form: named behaviors per pilot, a commitment problem that needed fixing, and an anti-"statically circling" behavior. Its use of player telemetry was to author new move sets, not to train a policy (as far as the reports say).
- Elite 2.1 is the cautionary tale for "skilled AI": a steep skill curve combined with stat advantages (engineered weapons) read as unfair, and the fix removed the stats, not the behavior. Competence should come from decisions, not numbers the player can't have.
- The Everspace tip confirms that in 6DoF games with strafe, a spiral circle-strafe is a dominant player tactic against fighter AI — the same exploit this game's player uses (circling while firing inward). The FS2 anti-circling and anti-stall rules exist for exactly this.
- Transfer: Star Citizen-style named behaviors and WC-style per-ace "thinking" transfer well; Ace Combat-style superhuman maneuvers do not fit a "same ship, same controls" premise.

### Gaps
- No primary developer material found on the dogfight AI of Star Wars: Squadrons, Rogue Squadron, Everspace 1/2, House of the Dying Sun (one-person studio, Mike Tipul, ex-Bungie per [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Companies/Marauder_Interactive)) or Rebel Galaxy Outlaw's enemies. A search summary claimed reviewers called Squadrons' single-player AI its weakest part (TIEs flying into asteroids), but the two reviews I checked (Gamepressure, Kotaku) did not say this; treat as unverified.
- No CEDEC/GDC talk on Ace Combat enemy AI was found (the CEDEC 2019 AC7 talk was about narrative).
- TIE Fighter/X-Wing rank parameters remain undocumented; XWVM's empirical reconstructions are behind a Bitbucket login.
- A search summary claimed Star Citizen pilot skill changes maneuver choice probabilities and parameters (braking/evade duration); I could not find the source report.

## Q3. Top-down / planar games with decoupled facing (Starsector, Endless Sky, Naev, Star Control 2/UQM, SPAZ, Reassembly, Cosmoteer, Escape Velocity, Subspace/Continuum, Nova Drift): how do they decide to approach, strafe, orbit, kite, retreat and fire?

### Takeaway
The open-source planar AIs converge on the same shape: choose a stance from a matchup comparison (my range/speed vs his, my HP×DPS vs his, is he targeting me), then run a short scripted pattern for that stance (approach → close-range aim-and-fire; flyby/hit-and-run; kite while aiming backward; stabilize-then-fire strafe; orbit), with personalities or flags biasing stance choice and skill gating the "fancy" maneuvers (zigzag, careful facing, dodging). Endless Sky, the most widely played open-source example, literally parks at 75% of weapon range and only rotates to aim — the "get in range and sit there" failure this project already has.

### Cited Findings
**Naev (Lua task-stack AI)**
- Attack style chosen per ship class (`atk.choose`: lighter / medium / capital / generic) into files `fighter.lua`, `bomber.lua`, `corvette.lua`, `capital.lua`, `drone.lua`, `generic.lua`, `pacifist.lua`, `util.lua`. — [Naev dat/ai/core/attack](https://github.com/naev/naev/tree/main/dat/ai/core/attack)
- Fighter AI: if beyond `range * atk_approach` with missile ammo → `atk.ranged`; otherwise `space_sup` (dogfight) for targets under 200 mass or `flyby` for heavier ones; 30% chance per think to reconsider target, none if close to current target. — [Naev fighter.lua](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/fighter.lua)
- `space_sup` ("designed for fighters engaging other fighters"): beyond range, turn then accelerate when within 10°; 0.8–1.0×range, face intercept and accelerate to close; 0.4–0.8×range, aim and fire everything, accelerate only "if it will be productive" (aligned within 15° and closing); inside 0.4×range, aim and fire. — [Naev util.lua L291–343](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- `flyby` ("a sequence of close-in flyby attacks... cruises by the target"): >3×range approach; 0.75–3×range keep facing and accelerate or turn while accelerating; close, aim and fire, and accelerate only if `mem.aggressive` — comment: "not accelerating here is the only difference between the aggression levels." — [Naev util.lua L230–284](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- `atk.ranged` picks one of three sub-patterns: dogfight if relative HP×DPS ≥ 0.25, or own missiles slower than 1.2× target top speed, or missile range < 1.5× gun range; else if the target is targeting me and in range: dogfight if contact is imminent, otherwise "Getting chased, try to kite"; else "Enemy is distracted, try to strafe and harass without engaging." — [Naev util.lua L480–509](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- Kite: first get the velocity vector pointing away from the enemy at ≥80% top speed, then "aim instead of facing" and fire while fleeing. Strafe: "place himself at range and at constant velocity. When he is stabilized, he starts shooting until he has to correct his trajectory again", with a hysteresis flag (`mustapproach`) and a give-up timer if missiles keep missing. — [Naev util.lua L373–476](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- Skill gates maneuvers: zigzag evasion is skipped when `mem.atk_skill <= 0.45+0.55*mem.rand` ("Some AI will not do fancy manoeuvres"); zigzag triggers only when out of range, nose and velocity both within 10° of the target, and incoming projectiles exist. The same skill test chooses `ai.face` vs `ai.careful_face`. — [Naev util.lua L131–150, L345–351](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- Target preference functions (`prefer_similar`, `prefer_capship`, `prefer_weaker`) score candidates with a 0.9–1.1 random multiplier "for less consistency"; a TODO asks about "statistical sampling instead of determinism". — [Naev util.lua L511–595](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)

**Endless Sky (C++ AI.cpp)**
- `Attack`: "Ramming AI doesn't take weapon range or self-damage into account"; otherwise `isAbleToRun = target.MaxVelocity() * SAFETY_MULTIPLIER < ship.MaxVelocity()` — "Have a 10% minimum to avoid ships getting in a chase loop." Ships with mostly long-range or blast weapons keep a minimum safe distance (backing off via reverse thrust or by turning away); otherwise, "Fire if we can or move closer": if within 0.75× shortest weapon range → `AimToAttack` (turn toward lead point only, no thrust); else `MoveToAttack`. — [Endless Sky AI.cpp L2934–3013](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- `MoveToAttack` aims at the lead point and thrusts only if within 90° and farther than its current turning diameter ("far enough away not to overshoot"), or if moving away while facing mostly toward the target; afterburner beyond 600 units. — [Endless Sky AI.cpp L3024–3054](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- `TargetAim` groups fixed guns by projectile velocity, computes an intercept per group, and aims with the group of highest expected DPS, preferring ammo-using or ready secondaries. — [Endless Sky AI.cpp L3753–3815](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- Personalities are flags (timid, daring, coward, heroic, nemesis, vindictive, merciful, plunders, swarming, ramming, opportunistic, etc.) that mainly alter target selection and flee thresholds (flee below 25% health, more for cowards); target selection deprioritizes a foe "if more than a quarter of your allies' strength is already attacking." — [Endless Sky AI.cpp](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- `CircleAround` (orbit) and `KeepStation` (position+velocity matching with deadbands and turn-jitter damping: "Avoid 'turn jitter' when position & velocity are well-matched") exist as reusable primitives. — [Endless Sky AI.cpp L2806–2930](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)

**Star Control II / The Ur-Quan Masters (Super Melee "cyborg")**
- `tactical_intelligence` fills up to 10 "objects of concern" (enemy ship, enemy weapon, gravity well, crew, misc), each tagged with a move state: `PURSUE`, `ENTICE` (hold distance, lure), `AVOID`, `NO_MOVEMENT`; the generic `ship_intelligence` then turns/thrusts/fires. — [UQM cyborg.c (MegaMod fork)](https://github.com/Serosis/UQM-MegaMod/blob/master/src/uqm/cyborg.c)
- Stance is a matchup test: `PURSUE` if the target is massive, or my weapon is short-range, or he has long-range seeking weapons, or (he out-thrusts me and out-ranges me); otherwise `ENTICE`. Pursuit includes "catch him on the back side" prediction and a give-up threshold (24 turns; 44 for slow heavy ships chasing the Earthling). A per-ship "ManeuverabilityIndex" = thrust × increment / (turn_wait + thrust_wait) classifies fast/medium/slow. — [UQM cyborg.c L1195–1213 and Pursue/Entice](https://github.com/Serosis/UQM-MegaMod/blob/master/src/uqm/cyborg.c)
- Difficulty: aim margin of error 0 for "Awesome", 20 px for "Good", 40 px for "Standard"; Standard disables the ship's special entirely; only Awesome dodges non-seeking projectiles via trajectory intersection (`PlotIntercept`). — [UQM cyborg.c L447–451, L1062–1063, L1282–1290](https://github.com/Serosis/UQM-MegaMod/blob/master/src/uqm/cyborg.c)
- Each of the ~25 ships has its own `*_intelligence` that calls the generic routine and then overrides it, e.g. Spathi clears the special and fires its rear B.U.T.T. missile only when the enemy is within 24 turns and behind. — [UQM spathi.c](https://github.com/Serosis/UQM-MegaMod/blob/master/src/uqm/ships/spathi/spathi.c)

### Inferences
- Recurring planar stance set: approach, dogfight-close, flyby/hit-and-run, kite (velocity away, nose back), strafe/harass at stable range, orbit, back off/vent, flee. Every one is cheap for a decoupled-facing ship (kite and strafe are native, not the contortions they are for Newtonian-nose-forward ships). This supports the user's hypothesis of a small named vocabulary.
- Stance choice from matchup is the shared core: UQM (range/thrust/mass comparison), Naev (relative HP×DPS, missile speed vs target speed, "is he targeting me"), Endless Sky (can I outrun him by 10%, long-range weapons?), Starsector (personality-preferred range + flux state). The "is he targeting me" and "am I being chased" inputs are what let Naev choose kite vs harass — directly relevant to "ignore each other / cannot anticipate the player".
- Endless Sky's `AimToAttack` (within 0.75× range: rotate only, no thrust) is a documented instance of "get in range and sit there". It is acceptable in ES because ES fights are fleet-scale and slow; for a duel-focused game it produces exactly the circle-and-fire exploit. Naev's `space_sup` has the same structure but keeps closing at mid-range.
- UQM's "generic AI + per-ship override" pattern maps to "generic maneuver layer + per-pilot/per-weapon overrides" (e.g. grenade drop only when pursuer is behind within N, as Spathi does with its rear missile — a near-exact analogue of this game's backward grenades).
- Difficulty in UQM and Reassembly is expressed as aim error and maneuver availability (`BAD_AIM`, `DODGES`, special disabled), not as stat cheats — consistent with "same ship as player".
- Transfer: very high for Naev, UQM and Reassembly flags (planar, decoupled or near-decoupled, small codebases a solo dev can read in an afternoon). Starsector's flux-driven back-off maps onto this game's laser heat: "back off at X% heat, re-engage at Y%" is a ready-made, readable personality knob.

- No concrete Subspace/Continuum bot dogfight logic was found.

## Q4. Which games are praised or criticized for enemy pilots, and for what?

### Takeaway
Praise attaches to FreeSpace (competent wingmen), and implicitly to systems with readable personalities (Starsector officers, Wing Commander aces). Criticism clusters into four failure modes: jousting/endless circling, passivity or static circling, unfairness/cheating (Elite 2.1, Ace Combat), and opaque inconsistency (original X-Wing AI varied with framerate).

### Cited Findings
- FreeSpace praised for "competent AI wingmen." — [Wikipedia: Descent: FreeSpace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent:_FreeSpace_%E2%80%93_The_Great_War)
- Jousting: Squadron: Mercenaries' original demo "suffered from 'space jousting'" until named maneuvers were added. — [Squadron: Mercenaries devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)
- Endless circling recognized in code: FS2 `SM_GET_AWAY` "to prevent endless circling"; stalemate detection added later by SCP. — [fs2open aicode.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp)
- Static circling recognized as a defect: Star Citizen's "punishidle" change so AI performs maneuvers "instead of statically circling the target." — [SC Monthly Report Aug 2025](https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/20753)
- Chase loops recognized in code: Endless Sky requires a 10% speed margin "to avoid ships getting in a chase loop." — [Endless Sky AI.cpp](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- Unfairness: Elite 2.1 NPCs perceived as overly aggressive and overpowered; fix stripped stat advantages. — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/elite-dangerous-patch-stops-ai-developing-wmds)
- Cheating perception: AC7 players suspect the AI never loses energy and out-turns them; ace fights "drag on" (player speculation). — [Steam AC7 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/502500/discussions/0/1840188800799010810)
- Starsector community notes AI weaknesses: "flux locking" (high-flux ships refuse to drop shields and cannot vent), aggressive personality backing off too late on slow ships, fleeing derelicts. — [Starsector wiki: AI Behaviour](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/AI_Behaviour)

### Inferences
- The user's complaints map onto these documented failure modes: "get in range and sit there" = Endless Sky's aim-only band and Star Citizen's static circling; "player wins by circling" = the stalemate FS2 added get-away and stalemate-breaking for; "timid" = missing FS2-style forced re-attack timer.
- Players tolerate AI that is better than them at decisions but resent AI that has different physics or stats; the "same ship and controls" premise is an asset to protect.

### Gaps
- No systematic critic/player survey of "which space-combat AI feels best" was found; praise evidence is anecdotal or encyclopedic.

## Q5. Is there a recurring architecture across the praised ones? Any shipped ML pilots?

### Takeaway
Yes: (1) target selection with anti-dogpile weighting, (2) a stance/maneuver layer of a handful of named, time-committed patterns chosen by aspect/range/matchup rules with randomness, (3) a steering-and-aim primitive underneath, (4) per-pilot numeric parameters plus personality flags, (5) aim error that shrinks with tracking time, and (6) explicit anti-stall rules (forced re-attack, get-away, stalemate breaker). No shipped space/flight-combat game was found that uses a learned policy for pilot tactics; the shipped ML examples use learning for low-level motion under authored tactics.

### Cited Findings
- FS2: target assignment with attacker penalty → chase submodes with timed commitment and dice → `ai_turn_towards_vector` / accel primitives → per-class/per-skill arrays → tracking-time aim error → 6 s forced super-attack and get-away. — [fs2open aicode.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp); [ai_profiles.tbl](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/def_files/ai_profiles.tbl)
- Naev: task stack (`ai.pushtask`, `ai.poptask`, `ai.pushsubtask("_attack_zigzag")`) over stance functions over `ai.face`/`ai.aim`/`ai.accel` primitives, with `mem.atk_skill` and `mem.aggressive` per pilot. — [Naev util.lua](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)
- UQM: concern evaluation → PURSUE/ENTICE/AVOID → generic turn/thrust/fire + per-ship override; difficulty via margin of error. — [UQM cyborg.c](https://github.com/Serosis/UQM-MegaMod/blob/master/src/uqm/cyborg.c)
- Squadron: Mercenaries: HSM for missions + decision tree in dogfights + data-driven maneuvers restrictable by skill. — [devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)
- ML, military sim (not a game): DARPA AlphaDogfight 2020 — Heron Systems' RL agent beat a human F-16 pilot 5-0 in simulation; it won largely with head-on gun shots, an approach human pilots don't normally use; commentators cautioned about simulation-specific conditions. — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/story/technology/darpa-artificial-intelligence-fighter-jet-dogfighting/); [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2020/08/24/lets-forget-the-hype-and-talk-about-the-ai-vs-human-pilot-simulated-dogfight/)
- ML, shipped (not flight): ARC Raiders (Embark, Oct 2025) uses RL for robot locomotion and some reactive behaviors, while tactical decisions (attacking, flanking, cover) run on behavior trees; GDC session "Learning to Move: Physics-Based Enemy Locomotion in ARC Raiders." — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-and-ai-what-the-game-actually-uses-and-why-its-controversial/); [GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/learning-to-move-physics-based-enemy-locomotion-in-arc-raiders/917319)
- Star Citizen used player combat data to design new AI move sets (not stated to be ML). — [DSOGaming on 3.23](https://dsogaming.com/?p=179491)
- Subspace Reticulum: transformer-driven NPCs in a 2D Subspace-like game, unreleased proof of concept, no evaluation published. — [itch.io](https://thisonethatone.itch.io/subspace-reticulum)

### Inferences
- The project's current split (authored layer above, MPC executing) matches the shipped pattern; ARC Raiders is the closest shipped precedent for "learned or optimized low level, authored tactics above". The AlphaDogfight result illustrates the RL risk: a policy can find a winning but unfun/unreadable strategy (head-on gunnery), which is the opposite of "readable, believable mistakes".
- The user's hypothesis (small vocabulary of committed named maneuvers, sequenced by an authored layer above the MPC) is essentially what FS2, Naev, UQM, Star Citizen and Squadron: Mercenaries do. The concrete additions the evidence suggests: per-maneuver minimum/maximum durations and exit conditions; a forced re-engage timer; a stalemate detector that triggers a disengage-and-re-enter maneuver; a matchup-based stance choice; tracking-time aim convergence; attacker caps / target spreading for multi-enemy coordination.
- Coordination in all of these is implicit (target-assignment penalties, attacker caps, "call for help" flags); none of the open-source planar AIs found do explicit pincer/bracketing tactics. Bracketing would be new authoring.

### Gaps
- No shipped space or flight combat game with an ML-trained pilot policy was found. Racing (e.g. Gran Turismo Sophy) was out of scope and not researched here.

## Q6. What do developers say about authoring cost, tuning and debugging?

### Takeaway
Direct statements are rare; the evidence is mostly structural. Hand-authored rule systems accumulate threshold soup (FS2's chase selector is dozens of magic numbers), get patched incrementally over decades, and are hard to reproduce even by dedicated fans when behavior depends on framerate. Data-driven parameter tables and flags were the main tuning tools; commitment and anti-circling fixes recur late in development.

### Cited Findings
- FS2's submode selector is a chain of hand-tuned thresholds (e.g. 0.2/0.5/0.8/0.9 dot cutoffs, 50/100/150/200/300/500 m, 2/3/4/5/6/8 s timers) with dated author comments from 1997–1998 and later SCP patches; behaviors such as stalemate breaking, glide attack and circle strafe were bolted on later as table-driven, default-off options. — [fs2open aicode.cpp](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/aicode.cpp); [ai_profiles.tbl](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/def_files/ai_profiles.tbl)
- FS2 exposes nearly all tuning as per-difficulty tables (ai_profiles) and per-class tables (ai.tbl) so mission designers tune without code. — [fs2open ai.h](https://github.com/scp-fs2open/fs2open.github.com/blob/master/code/ai/ai.h)
- XWVM: rank behavior had to be reverse-engineered from observation, required a ground-up AI rewrite, and can never match 1:1 because the original was framerate- and platform-dependent. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/star-wars-fans-recreate-1993s-xwing/)
- Star Citizen: "detailed investigations were made into the current state of AI ship combat" leading to tactic-commitment improvements, years into development. — [SC Monthly Report Aug 2025](https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/20753)
- Squadron: Mercenaries credits the data-driven decomposition with easy addition of maneuvers and skill restrictions. — [devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)
- Endless Sky code admits approximations in place ("This isn't perfect, but it works well enough.") and carries TODOs around formation state. — [Endless Sky AI.cpp](https://github.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/blob/master/source/AI.cpp)
- Naev's own TODO questions whether target choice should be statistical sampling rather than deterministic. — [Naev util.lua](https://github.com/naev/naev/blob/main/dat/ai/core/attack/util.lua)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer, the cheapest debuggable form is: named maneuvers as data (entry conditions, commit duration, exit conditions, MPC goal shape), a visible current-maneuver label per pilot (for readability and debugging), and per-pilot parameter sheets (accuracy, reaction delay, evasion, courage/heat back-off, patience). This is the FS2/Reassembly/Starsector pattern minus the per-frame threshold soup.
- Framerate dependence (XWVM) is a concrete warning: maneuver timers and dice rolls should run on fixed-step simulation time, which also helps reproducible tests.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk, Game AI Pro chapter or Game Developer postmortem specifically on space-dogfight AI was found in this pass (gameaipro.com was not surfaced by search; searches returned DARPA material instead).
- No quantitative authoring-cost statements (person-months, iteration counts) were found for any title.
